# Box2D pool sharing

Throwaway spike of the physics research, whose second stage settles how a physics library with a task interface shares the engine's workers. It measures, instead of assuming, what driving Box2D v3.2.0 from a scheduler shaped like the engine's frame pool costs against the library's own scheduler at the same worker count, on dedicated cores and on oversubscribed runners, and whether a dedicated subset of the team is a better policy where cores are shared with the engine's other work. The engine's concurrency model sets the threshold this spike measures against: the component's step on the engine's pool costs no more than its step on its native scheduler by more than 10 percent on dedicated cores, and scales on the oversubscribed cells no worse than the engine's own nodes do.

## Hypothesis

With Box2D v3.2.0 built as its author prescribes (contraction into fused multiply-add disabled, no fast math), on the author's benchmark scenes:

1. The hashes are the worker sweep spike's on every cell: the adapter changes no result, whatever the team, the policy or the cell.
2. On dedicated cores (the founder's machine, 16 cores) the engine-shaped scheduler's step time, the whole team claiming the library's tasks, is within 10 percent of the native scheduler's at every worker count.
3. On the oversubscribed cells (8 workers on 4 virtual CPUs) the engine-shaped scheduler loses no more over its 4-worker time than the engine's own nodes do (5 to 7 percent, the scheduling overhead spike).
4. A dedicated-subset variant, the library given half the team while the other half runs a synthetic parallel pass of the reference workload's kind, is reported beside the whole-team variant, so that the physics proposal can choose per machine class.

Refuting thresholds: a hash difference; a whole-team cost above 10 percent on dedicated cores.

## Method

A C17 driver (the worker sweep spike's driver, its hash and its scenes) fetches Box2D at tag `v3.2.0` through CMake and compiles the library's own shared scenes from the fetched tree. The engine-shaped pool is a C++17 library behind the two callbacks Box2D expects: a fixed team of workers, the stepping thread counted as worker 0 so that the team equals Box2D's worker count; the library's tasks published one by one into a ring and claimed under one central counter; a worker that finds nothing spins for at most 50 microseconds, then parks on a condition variable with the lost-wake-up check done under the lock; the finish call helps with the library's pending tasks only, then waits for its task, spinning the same bound before parking; at one worker the enqueue runs the task inline and tells the library so. The dedicated-subset variant gives the library half the team (Box2D reads that count as its worker count) while the other half runs, for the duration of every step, a synthetic chunked pass of the reference workload's kind (a damped spring integrated over 256-row chunks claimed under a central counter, each worker on rows of its own) and parks between steps.

The driver runs the seven benchmark scenes (`joint_grid`, `large_pyramid`, `many_pyramids`, `rain`, `smash`, `spinner`, `tumbler`) with the step counts of the author's benchmark program, at 1/60 s with 4 substeps, for every combination of scheduler (native, engine-shaped with the whole team, engine-shaped with the dedicated subset from two workers up) and team size (1, 2, 4, 8, plus 16 and the machine's own logical processor count where it has more than 8). Every configuration runs three times and the fastest run is kept, the hashes of the rounds compared, since a single multithreaded run's time varies by tens of percent on a desktop machine. After every step the driver hashes every body transform as the worker sweep does (a broadphase query covering the scene, bodies sorted by stored id, position and rotation hashed field by field with FNV-1a 64) and records the final hash, a chain hash of all step hashes, whether the final hash equals the one the worker sweep measured for the scene, the first step at which the run diverges from the native run at one worker, the step times (total, median, maximum; the hashing happens outside the timed region), the ratio of its total to the native run's at the same team and to the native run's at its library worker count, the tasks the library enqueued and who ran them, the parks of the workers and of the finish call, and the synthetic chunks per step of the other half. `--self-check` verifies the pool's and the driver's own logic (tasks run exactly once and the finish returns only once its task is done over several steps at several team shapes; the synthetic half runs during the steps and not between them; two identical serial runs; the pool at four workers, whole team and dedicated subset, reaching the serial hashes; the one-worker inline path) and `--quick` runs a tenth of the steps in one round. `ci/run-spike.sh box2d-pool-sharing <cell>` builds it with the platform's default compilers, runs it and saves `facts.json`, `environment.txt` and the CMake configure output.

Relationship to the external component: Box2D v3.2.0 (MIT) is the subject of the measurement; nothing of it is vendored or reused beyond this spike. The engine-shaped pool is the spike's own and borrows the shape of the engine's concurrency model (the central counter, the bounded spin then park, the finish that helps the library only); it is evidence about that shape, not a component of the engine.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=box2d-pool-sharing`. Release build with the default C and C++ compilers of each image, `-ffp-contract=off` on GCC and Clang for every target of the build, Box2D's own CMake flags otherwise. Plus a local run on the founder's machine (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 logical processors, Windows 11, MSVC 19.51) outside the matrix, which gives the dedicated-cores figure of hypothesis 2.

## Result

Local run of 2026-11-09 on the founder's machine (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 logical processors in two core complexes, Windows 11, MSVC 19.51), three rounds per configuration, the fastest kept:

- Hypothesis 1 holds on this machine: the 119 runs (7 scenes; teams of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32; native, whole team, dedicated subset from two workers up) all give the final hash the worker sweep measured for their scene, no run diverges from the native one-worker run at any step, and the three rounds of every configuration agree.
- Hypothesis 2, the whole team against the native scheduler at the same team, best of three rounds: within 4 percent at 1 worker (0.99 to 1.04), 2 percent at 2 (0.97 to 1.02), 6 percent at 16 (1.00 to 1.06) and 3 percent at 32 (0.98 to 1.03), on every scene. At 4 and 8 workers the ratios spread from 0.61 to 1.53 in both directions, and the rounds of one configuration differ by up to 1.7 times (the tumbler's native run at 4 workers: 339, 583 and 582 ms; the whole-team run at 8: 223, 296 and 174 ms), while the rounds at 1, 2, 16 and 32 workers agree within a few percent: on this machine a team of 4 or 8 lands on one core complex or across the two, with the stacked cache or without, and the placement, not the scheduler, decides the time. The threshold is met where the measurement is stable; at 4 and 8 workers this machine cannot judge a 10 percent difference, and the cells' figures are the other data point.
- Hypothesis 4, the dedicated subset against the native scheduler at the same library worker count (half the team for the library, the other half on the synthetic pass): 1.27 to 1.58 with 1 library worker beside 1 synthetic worker, 0.95 to 1.96 with 2 beside 2, 0.73 to 1.29 with 4 beside 4, 0.82 to 1.43 with 8 beside 8, and 1.15 to 1.56 with 16 beside 16, which fills every logical processor. Reading: the other half's work is not free for the library even on a 16-core machine, since it competes for the shared cache and, from 16 library workers up, for the physical cores; against the native scheduler at the whole team the subset costs 1.07 to 2.63, which is the price of giving the library half the workers.
- The pool's own figures: the library enqueues 3 to 6 tasks per step at 1 worker and up to about 40 at 32; the workers park about once per step per worker at 2 to 8 workers and several times per step per worker at 32 (up to 76 000 parks over the rain's 1 000 steps at 32 workers); the stepping thread parks in finish between 0 and 0.4 times per step; the synthetic half runs 16 000 to 110 000 chunks of 256 rows per step.

Native scheduler totals, best of three, at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 workers: `large_pyramid` 554, 478, 308, 283, 224, 339 ms; `joint_grid` 1 624, 1 400, 1 025, 632, 490, 503; `rain` 4 247, 2 317, 2 607, 1 454, 1 027, 1 151; `smash` 595, 462, 339, 317, 236, 300; `tumbler` 617, 487, 339, 237, 281, 398; the engine-shaped whole team: `large_pyramid` 547, 473, 306, 174, 228, 345; `joint_grid` 1 683, 1 411, 927, 549, 499, 509; `rain` 4 191, 2 320, 2 669, 1 507, 1 024, 1 171; `smash` 596, 469, 342, 250, 247, 295; `tumbler` 614, 475, 364, 362, 298, 410. Both schedulers stop scaling at 16 workers and lose at 32 (the logical processors beyond the 16 cores), the library's own limit on these scenes.
