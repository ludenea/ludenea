# Reference workload history capture

Throwaway spike of the control surface research. It carries, over the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) with kicks as inputs and wall bounces as events, the smallest always-on capture from which causal queries are computable later (the per-tick input log, the per-tick hash, the event log and a snapshot on a cadence), and measures, instead of assuming, whether a world restored from any snapshot and replayed from the log reaches the live run's hashes and events at every worker count, what the capture costs per tick at cadences of 60, 10 and 1, how long a causal query on one entity takes by instrumented replay on a harness world, whether a live world stepped by a paced host is affected by queries running beside it, what the capture retains in memory against the arithmetic of the proposal, and whether a tick outside retention is a structured error.

## Layout

- `src/world.rs`: the [control surface toy](../control-surface-toy/README.md) spike's world (the reference workload on the dependency-counting tick graph, with the input fixed point that applies a tick's record of kicks before its first system) plus what the capture needs: the snapshot of the [runtime skeleton](../runtime-skeleton/README.md) spike (the canonical bytes of a tick boundary, restored into a new world), the wall-bounce events of the [frame allocator](../workload-frame-allocator/README.md) spike (recorded by the motion chunks, merged in chunk order at the fixed point, returned per tick), and an instrumentation of one watched entity for the harness world of a causal query: after every node of every tick, the watched entity's row is read once, with the kicks that reached it, the neighbors within the interaction radius whose positions the repulsion read, the velocity the repulsion wrote, the row the motion wrote with the walls it bounced on, and the row after the fixed point.
- `src/capture.rs`: the capture. A record per tick (the kicks as canonical bytes, the hash, the events), a snapshot every `cadence` ticks, a retention window that drops the snapshots older than the window and the records nobody can replay from. A causal query plans a replay (the nearest snapshot at or before the tick and the records up to it, cloned so that the replay runs outside any lock), restores a harness world watching the entity, replays, checks the replayed hash against the recorded one, and answers with the chain as data: per tick, the kicks on the entity, its neighbors, the systems that changed which of its components (input, repulsion, motion, structural changes), the bounces and the state after. A tick older than the retained snapshots, in the future, or whose records are incomplete is a structured error with a code and a context.
- `src/main.rs`: the scenarios and the report.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander system off, the script's kicks (every tenth tick, the sixteen watched entities toward the center of the world) as inputs, compiled by one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features:

1. A world restored from the snapshot at tick 60 times n and replayed from the input log reaches the live run's hash at every tick up to the next snapshot, and emits the live run's events, for every n (sixteen windows), at 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers, on every cell.
2. The always-on cost (the log append, the hash record, the event log append, the snapshot at a cadence of 60) is under 2 percent of the run at 4 workers; at a cadence of 10 it stays under 5 percent; at a cadence of 1 (the rollback configuration, stepped one tick at a time) it is the snapshot's cost, 10 to 40 percent of the tick as the proposal's arithmetic predicted.
3. A causal query on one entity at a tick (which systems changed which of its components since the previous snapshot, from which kicks, neighbors and walls) is answered by an instrumented replay on a harness world in under 100 milliseconds on the runners, for a replay of up to 60 ticks, with the replayed hash equal to the recorded one; while queries run, a live world stepped by a paced host (one tick per millisecond) keeps its hashes (equal to a direct run's) and its step time within the noise.
4. The memory of the capture (the snapshots times their count plus the logs) matches, within 10 percent, the arithmetic of the proposal from the skeleton spike's snapshot size, the toy spike's log bytes per tick and the frame allocator spike's events per tick.
5. A query at a tick older than the retained snapshots is a structured error naming the tick, the oldest answerable tick and the window, while a tick inside the window is answered; a query at a snapshot's own tick replays nothing.

## Method

The live run: a world at 4 workers (the machine's parallelism when smaller) stepped to 1 000 ticks in batches of ten with the script's kicks, captured at a cadence of 60 with no retention window, after a warm-up instance. The replays: from every snapshot to the next (the last to tick 1 000), at 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers, the hashes compared tick by tick with the recorded ones and the events with the recorded events. The cost: live runs at 1 and 4 workers for five configurations (no snapshot in batches of ten; cadence 60 and cadence 10 in batches of ten; no snapshot and cadence 1 one tick at a time), the capture's own time per component measured around each call and reported as a share of the run beside the world's stepping time. The queries: twenty causal queries on the completed capture at ticks spread from the first snapshot on, with the harness world at 1 and 4 workers, their latency split into restore and replay; then a paced host stepping a live world one tick per millisecond (capture at cadence 60, retention 600 ticks) alone and with queries issued every 20 milliseconds seven ticks behind the live tick, with the harness at 4 workers then at 1, the live hashes compared with a direct run's; at the end of the paced run, a query inside the window and one just outside it. The memory: the retained bytes of the cadence 60 run against the prediction, and the ten-minute extrapolation at 60 ticks per second.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-history-capture`) verify on the small size that the world without kicks reproduces the sequential reference and with kicks agrees with a sequential oracle on hashes and events at 1, 2, 3 and 8 workers in batches of 1, 7 and 40, that a snapshot restores into a world that continues identically, that a watched entity is traced after every node without changing the hashes (the traced position follows from the traced velocity, the neighbors equal the grid's), that snapshots fall on the cadence and the replay from each reaches the live hashes and events, that the retention window drops old snapshots and names what it cannot answer, that a causal query replays the chain of a kicked entity and detects a corrupt record by the hash, and that the whole report runs with every check true.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-history-capture`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a local run on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26) outside the matrix. The runners have 4 virtual CPUs (3 on macOS): a harness world at 4 workers beside a live world at 4 shares them. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only.
