# Reference workload test harness

Throwaway spike of the game testing research. It runs the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) as tests of the standard Rust test harness, each test stepping two isolated worlds of one process from one fixture at two worker counts, and measures, instead of assuming, whether such a test reports no divergence when there is none and the first divergent tick and system when a worker-count-dependent bug is injected, whether sixteen tests run in parallel in one process give the hashes each gives alone and what the suite's wall time is against the tests alone, what a fixture restored from a snapshot costs, whether a test whose world panics fails alone with its report, and whether the same tests pass unchanged on the build without and with the presentation capability.

## Layout

- `src/world.rs`: the world, a trimmed copy of the [runtime skeleton](../runtime-skeleton/README.md) spike's core (the reference workload on the dependency-counting tick graph, stepped by whole ticks, with the snapshot restored into a new world, the panic caught at the task boundary that fails its world, and the extraction extension point), plus two additions for the tests: a divergence injection, which from a chosen tick on computes the energy as a free-running reduction (each worker accumulates the sums of the motion chunks it happened to run and the fixed point adds the workers' partials, the construct the [worker sweep](../workload-worker-sweep/README.md) spike measured against the deterministic one), and the hashes of the component groups the systems write (the velocities of the repulsion and the motion, the positions of the motion, the energy of the energy reduction, the identifiers, liveness, masses and stream of the structural changes), computed only at a divergent tick so that a report names the first system that diverged.
- `src/lib.rs`: what a test needs: the settings read once from the environment (the size, the report directory, whether the failing test is armed), the shared fixture (the sequential reference run and the snapshot of the world at tick 0, computed once per process), the test case and the comparison (two worlds restored from the fixture, stepped to the end in batches, every completed tick's hash compared, and at the first divergent tick the two snapshots and the group hashes taken), the report a test writes as JSON, and the presentation stub (the skeleton spike's extraction node, registered by the fixture when the `presentation` feature is on).
- `tests/divergence.rs`: the sixteen tests of the standard harness. Eight without an injection, at the worker pairs (1, 4), (1, 2), (2, 4), (4, 4), (1, 3), (2, 3), (3, 4) and (1, 1), expecting no divergence and the sequential reference's hashes from both worlds. Seven with the divergence injected in the second world from a stated tick, at (500; 1 and 4 workers), (250; 2 and 4), (750; 1 and 2), (100; 1 and 3), (900; 3 and 4), (1; 4 and 4) and (950; 1 and 4), expecting a divergence at or after the injected tick with the energy as the first and only divergent group and the two snapshots of that tick different. The sixteenth, armed by the orchestrator, injects a panic in the second chunk of the repulsion pass of the second world at tick 300 and fails alone with the failure's tick, node, chunk, worker and message.
- `src/main.rs`: the orchestrator the research CI runs. It builds the test executable in both configurations with the child `cargo`, runs the suite on the measured size alone, in parallel and with the failing test armed, collects the per-test reports and writes the measurements.

The worlds step on the skeleton spike's step-scoped workers, spawned at every step and joined before it returns: the persistent pool of the runtime proposal, shared by the worlds of one process, is a later measurement (the runtime research's loose end). The ticks of the injections are stated for the measured run of 1 000 ticks and scale to the 40 ticks of the self-check.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander system off, compiled by one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features:

1. A test creates two worlds from one fixture (the snapshot of the reference workload at tick 0), steps them to tick 1 000 at 1 and 4 workers and compares their per-tick hashes, and reports no divergence, both worlds giving the sequential reference's hashes, on every cell.
2. With a divergence injected in the second world from a chosen tick (the worker-count-dependent energy reduction), the test reports the first divergent tick and names the energy, the energy reduction's group, as the first divergent system, with no other group differing at that tick, and keeps the two snapshots of that tick, which differ. The reported tick is the injected one when the first injected pass was run by more than one worker; a pass short enough for one worker to run it entirely before the others wake (the motion pass costs tens of microseconds) groups the chunks as the reference does, so the report may name a later tick: the measurement counts, per cell, the tests whose reported tick equals the injected one and the largest lag.
3. With no injection, the test reports none at every worker pair, on every cell.
4. Sixteen such tests run in parallel in one process through the standard harness, each with its own worlds, and every test's hashes are the hashes it gives alone (both worlds of a test without an injection; the first world of a test with one matches the reference over the ticks it ran, its second world carrying the timing-dependent reduction on purpose and the comparison stopping at a first divergent tick that varies). The plan stated the suite's wall time as the longest test's within 25 percent on four cores, which holds only if the tests' total work fits the cores in the longest test's time; the measurement reports the suite's wall time, less the fixture's setup, three ways: the speedup over the solo run, the ratio to the ideal of the solo wall times spread over the cores (which a test at 4 workers, already using several cores alone, cannot reach) and the ratio to the ideal of the worker time (every world's stepping time times its worker count, an upper bound of the processor time) spread over the cores, plus the ratio to the longest test alone, at the machine's parallelism and with every test at once.
5. A fixture restored from the snapshot costs under 2 milliseconds per world for 10 000 entities on the cells, alone and under the load of the parallel runs, and a test whose world panics in a system fails alone, with the failure's context in its message and in its report, while the fifteen others pass.
6. The same tests pass unchanged on the build without the presentation capability and on the build with it, with the same hashes.

## Method

The orchestrator builds the integration test executable twice with `cargo test --no-run` in its own target directory (without the feature, and with `--features presentation`), reading the executable's path from the compiler's messages. On the build without the feature it runs the suite with `--test-threads=1` (the solo run, which gives each test's wall time alone), at the machine's parallelism, with every test at once (sixteen threads), and at the machine's parallelism with the failing test armed; on the build with the feature, once at the machine's parallelism. Each run gets a fresh report directory where every test writes its report as one flat JSON object: the worker counts, the injection tick, the batch, the final and run hashes of both worlds against the reference, the first divergent tick, the snapshots' sizes and first differing byte, the groups differing and the first divergent group and system, the restore microseconds of each world, the stepping and wall milliseconds, the extracted ticks and the shared fixture's setup time. A comparison without an injection steps in batches of 10 ticks; with an injection, one tick at a time, so that the worlds stand exactly at the first divergent tick when it is seen.

The orchestrator then checks that every test's run hashes are identical across the five runs, computes the suite's wall time per run minus the fixture's setup against the solo run, the two ideals and the longest solo test, the restore times' maximum and mean per run, the divergence counts per run (found, reported tick equal to the injected one, energy first, only the energy differing, snapshots different, the largest lag) and the armed run's outcome (the failed test, its message, the others passed). In a build with debug assertions the orchestrator measures the small size, since the dev profile's 16 tests of 1 000 ticks would take too long; the report says which size ran.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-test-harness`, and with `--features presentation`) run the sixteen tests on the small size (1 000 entities, 40 ticks) and the unit tests: the group hashes change only the group a change touches; the world without injection matches the reference at 1, 2 and 3 workers in batches of 1, 7 and 100; a world with the injection at 3 workers diverges from the reference after the injected tick and only in the energy group; the injection at 1 worker keeps the reference's order; the snapshot round trip is byte-identical and a corrupt snapshot is refused; a panic fails its world with its context; the compiler's messages and the harness's output are read correctly. At the small size the motion pass has four chunks that one worker often runs entirely before the others wake, so the injected tests accept a missing divergence there; the measured size demands it.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-test-harness`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile (the dev profile measures the small size), default target features of each target; plus a local run on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26) outside the matrix. The runners have 4 virtual CPUs (3 on macOS, and the x86_64 cells' four are two cores with their hyperthreads), which bounds the world-level parallelism they can show. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only.

## Result

Research CI [run](https://github.com/ludenea/ludenea/actions/runs/41708454407) of 2026-11-03 on the five cells (Intel Xeon Platinum 8370C on Linux x86_64, AMD EPYC 7763 on Windows x86_64, Neoverse N2 on Linux ARM64, Cobalt 100 on Windows ARM64, a virtual Apple M1 with 3 CPUs on macOS), and a local run the same day on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26) outside the matrix. On every cell the five runs of the suite behave alike: the sixteen tests pass in the solo run, the two parallel runs and the run with the presentation capability, the armed run fails its one test alone, every test's hashes are identical across the five runs and the two builds, and the seven injected tests report a divergence in all thirty runs with the energy as the first and only divergent group and two different snapshots of that tick. - Hypothesis 1 holds: the test at 1 and 4 workers, and the seven other worker pairs, report no divergence, both worlds giving the sequential reference's hashes, on every cell and in every run.
- Hypothesis 2 holds with the nuance the hypothesis states: in all thirty runs the seven injected tests find the divergence, name the energy as the first divergent group and the energy reduction as its system, find no other group differing at that tick, and keep two snapshots of that tick that differ at byte 250 029 of 250 041 (the energy). The reported tick equals the injected one in 2 to 5 of the 7 tests in the solo runs (the largest lag 1 or 2 ticks) and in 0 to 3 of 7 in the parallel runs, where the lag reaches 4 to 11 ticks on the Linux and macOS cells and the founder's machine and 38 to 105 ticks on the two Windows cells with four or sixteen tests competing for the virtual CPUs: a worker-count-dependent reduction only manifests when the pass is actually split between workers, and under load one worker often runs the whole motion pass (tens of microseconds) before the others wake. The report names the first tick at which the bug manifested, which the snapshots of that tick confirm; the injection tick is the bug's cause, not its first symptom.
- Hypothesis 3 holds: none of the eight tests without an injection reports a divergence, at any worker pair, on any cell, in any run.
- Hypothesis 4 holds for the hashes and not for the plan's wording of the wall time: every test's hashes are the hashes it gives alone, across the solo run, the two parallel runs and the build with the capability, on every cell. The suite runs 1.75 to 2.26 times faster than solo at the cells' CPU count (3.6 times on the founder's 10 cores) and no faster with all sixteen tests at once; against the ideal of the worker time spread over the cores it reaches 0.88 to 1.06 on the 4-CPU cells (0.77 on the macOS runner's 3, 1.42 on the founder's machine, whose efficiency cores count as cores), so the suite is bound by the processor time its worlds already consume at 4 workers and world-level parallelism cannot shorten it further; against the longest test alone it takes 3.6 to 5.0 times longer on the cells and 2.0 locally, so the plan's "within 25 percent of the longest test on four cores" does not hold for a suite whose total work is eight to nine times its longest test.
- Hypothesis 5 holds alone and not under load, and holds for the failure: the fixture's restore costs 0.34 to 0.81 ms on average and 0.37 to 0.80 ms at most in the solo runs on every machine but the macOS runner (3.95 ms at most there); with four tests restoring at once the maximum rises to 1.1 to 4.2 ms and with sixteen to 3.3 to 29.6 ms, the cost of sixteen restores competing for the cores, not of the restore. The armed run fails its one test alone on every cell (15 passed, 1 failed, a failing exit status), with the failure's tick (300), node (repulsion), chunk (2), worker and message in the harness output and in the test's report, the first world at tick 300 matching the reference.
- Hypothesis 6 holds: the build with the presentation capability passes the sixteen tests with the same hashes on every cell, the stub extraction node having run at every tick of every world.
- Beyond the hypotheses: the suite's fixture (the sequential reference run and the snapshot at tick 0) costs 1.0 to 2.1 s per process, paid once; the test executable builds in 1.0 to 3.7 s per configuration on top of the workspace's dependencies; the longest test is the one at 1 and 1 workers (2.1 to 4.3 s), two sequential worlds of 1 000 ticks.

The raw measurements and the environment of each cell are the artifacts of the run. The result holds for the reference workload version 1 at this toolchain and on these images.
