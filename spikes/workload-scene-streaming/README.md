# Scene streaming on a grid of cells

Throwaway spike of the scenes and levels research. It partitions the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md)'s entities into a planar grid of cells, each cell a scene instance whose identity is its grid coordinate, with references inside and across neighbouring cells, loaded and released by a scripted streaming policy from a point of interest moving along a scripted path, and measures, instead of assuming, whether the per-tick hashes of the streamed world are independent of the worker count and equal to a sequential oracle's at every tick, with a replay from the recorded input log reproducing a run whatever the loader's real delays; whether every reference to an entity of a cell that is not loaded reads the deterministic "not present" value and never a stale slot; whether the remap table's size and the cost of a load and an unload at the fixed point follow the loaded cells rather than the world's size (100 against 400 cells); what a leaving distance one cell larger than the entering distance saves in loads along a path that crosses cell borders back and forth; what the structural fixed point costs at a fast traversal with the spawn sliced at 500 entities per tick and unsliced; and whether two worlds of one process streaming the same grid from different paths each reproduce their own oracle with one shared payload store.

## Layout

- `src/grid.rs`: the grid (N by N cells of 512 units, so that 2 500 entities per cell is the reference workload's density and 250 a tenth of it), the payload of a cell generated from its coordinate and a seed (the import action as a function: per entity a local identifier, a position inside the cell, a velocity, a mass and a reference, one entity in ten referencing an entity of a neighbouring cell by the neighbour's coordinate and a local identifier drawn from the seed, one in a hundred an entity of its own cell), serialized as 32 canonical bytes per entity behind a 12-byte header with an FNV-1a content hash, so that no data file is checked in; the payload store shared read-only by the worlds of a process, importing a cell on its first request; the path of the point of interest (cell waypoints, one cell per a configurable number of ticks, the position a function of the tick so that every world computes the same bits, with the velocity); the spatial hash (the workload's counting sort over 16-unit bins covering the grid) and the kernels every pass and the oracle share (the workload's repulsion over those bins, the boundary bounce inside the entity's own cell).
- `src/streaming.rs`: the world parameters (entering and leaving distances in cells, the spawn slice, the scripted delay), the records of the input log (a load command, an unload command, an arrival) with their canonical bytes, the residency state of a cell (unloaded, requested, arrived, spawning, loaded, unloading: the meta record that outlives the payload), the default policy (every unloaded cell whose centre lies within the entering distance of the point is requested, the requests ordered by the direction of travel, the cells ahead first; every requested, arrived, spawning or loaded cell beyond the leaving distance is released; only the square of cells the leaving distance reaches is visited, plus the cells known to be resident), and the live loader (a dispatcher thread giving every request its own short-lived thread, as the late asset arrival spike's loader per arrival, each sleeping a real delay drawn from the clock between 2 and 20 milliseconds, fetching the payload from the store and posting the arrival to the world's queue).
- `src/state.rs`: the entity columns and the per-tick hash (FNV-1a over the canonical bytes slot by slot: the generation and the liveness, and for a live slot the cell, the local identifier, the position, the velocity, the mass and the observed word; then the energy and the point of interest), the instance records (residency per cell, the pending structural changes in record order, the arrived payloads, the remap table partitioned by instance, the input log) with the validation of every record (a load of a cell that is not unloaded, an arrival for a request the cell no longer awaits, an unload of a cell that is neither requested nor resident are deterministic rejections, logged), and the resolution of a full reference through the remap table (the target instance's partition, then the local identifier's runtime identifier, verified against the slot's generation, cell and local identifier: a slot that is not the target is a stale read, counted).
- `src/world.rs`: a trimmed copy of the [late asset arrival](../workload-asset-arrival/README.md) spike's world (itself a trimmed copy of the [runtime skeleton](../runtime-skeleton/README.md) spike's core: whole ticks on the dependency-counting tick graph, double buffered, the hash of a tick overlapping the next tick's passes, a panic caught at the task boundary) with the workload's kills and spawns replaced by the scene loads and unloads. A tick runs the spatial hashing (one task), the reference system (chunks, beside the spatial hashing: every live entity's reference resolved through the table against the state the passes read, the result written to the entity's observed word, the "not present" value, a packed position, or the no-reference value), the repulsion pass (chunks), the motion pass (chunks: integration, boundary inside the cell, energy per chunk), then the structural node alone: the lagging buffer learns the last structural changes, the pending loads and unloads are applied in record order with the spawn sliced by the world parameter (a spawn takes the lowest free slots, growing both buffers when none is free, fills its partition and enters the remap table at its last slice, the instance reading as absent until then; a despawn frees its slots in local order, bumps their generation and leaves the table), the point of interest advances along its path, the buffers swap, the policy writes its commands for the next tick, and the input fixed point of the next tick applies and logs its records (the commands, then the arrivals sorted by request identifier; a scripted loader delivers a requested payload a fixed number of ticks after the request; a replay applies the log's records of the tick in their order with the policy off). Every node's task time, the structural node's time per tick split into its parts, and every tick's wall time are instrumented.
- `src/oracle.rs`: the sequential oracle, the same schedule of records stepped single-threaded by a plain loop over one buffer, with the spawn, the despawn, the slot allocation and the slicing written again rather than shared with the graph, and an independent check of every reference read against the oracle's own residency (present if and only if the target cell's partition is complete, and never a stale slot).
- `src/main.rs` and `src/report.rs`: the scenarios, the report as one JSON document on standard output, the per-tick log written to `out/<cell>/workload-scene-streaming-ticks.log` (one section per run with its phase times, then per tick the hash, the structural node's and the structural changes' nanoseconds, the tick's wall nanoseconds, the entities spawned and despawned and the loaded cells), and the self-check that runs the whole report on the small grid.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload's components and kernels on the cells of a grid (cells of 512 units; 250 and 2 500 authored entities per cell; grids of 10 by 10 and 20 by 20 cells, 100 and 400 cells), the entering distance 2 cells and the leaving distance 3 (2 and 2 for the no-hysteresis control), the point of interest following a path of twelve moves of one cell with border crossings back and forth at one cell every 240 ticks (four seconds at the default step, Marvel's Spider-Man's 32 m/s over 128 m tiles; one cell every 60 ticks for the fast traversal control), the spawn sliced at 500 entities per tick, a scripted loader delivering 30 ticks after the request or the live loader's real delay, compiled by the pinned Rust toolchain at default target features, at 1, 4 and 8 workers:

1. **Determinism.** The per-tick hashes of the streamed world are identical at 1, 4 and 8 workers and across the five cells, equal to the oracle's at every tick, for both grid sizes, both densities and both speeds; a replay from the recorded input log (the policy's decisions and the arrivals, each with the tick it took effect at) reproduces the live run at every worker count whatever the loader's real delays. Refuted by a hash that differs across worker counts, cells or from the oracle, the sliced spawn included.
2. **"Not present".** Every cross-cell reference read while its target cell is absent, requested, spawning or unloaded gives the deterministic absent value, and every one read while the target is loaded gives the target's position; after an unload no reference resolves to a slot of the unloaded instance. The streamed world counts its reads while absent, while present and its stale slots; the oracle counts the same and checks every read against its own residency; the counts must agree and the stale slots and the check failures must be zero. Refuted by a stale slot, a check failure or counts that differ.
3. **The remap cost scales with the loaded instances, not with the world.** With the same entering and leaving distances, the remap table's maximum size and the time of a load and of an unload at the fixed point per cell differ by under 10 percent between the 100-cell and the 400-cell worlds at 2 500 entities per cell, on the founder's machine (the threshold the fundamental architecture proposal fixes for the remap against the load). The figure of a grid is the lowest median time per cell over three alternating rounds (the mean is reported beside it: a run despawns a few dozen cells of some microseconds each, so one preempted event moves the mean by half, as the first local run showed). Refuted by a cost or a table size that grows with the world's size at a fixed loaded count.
4. **Hysteresis.** With a leaving distance one cell larger than the entering distance, the scripted path triggers fewer loads than with equal distances, the saving at least a quarter of the loads along the path, with no effect on determinism (both runs equal their oracle). Refuted by a saving under a quarter or a hash that differs from the oracle.
5. **Cost.** At the fast traversal (one cell every 60 ticks) on the 400-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell, the structural fixed point's work (the spawns and despawns) stays under 5 percent of the tick's wall time at 4 workers when the spawn is sliced at 500 entities per tick; the unsliced spawn's peak tick is reported beside it, and the cost per loaded entity for the policy's budget. Refuted by a sliced share at or above 5 percent.
6. **Many worlds.** Two worlds of one process streaming the same 100-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell from two paths (the path and its mirror), stepped alternately from one thread with one shared payload store and one live loader, each reproduce their own oracle, the store holding one payload per requested cell with one import each. Refuted by a hash that differs or a cell imported twice.

## Method

The binary runs the matrix: for each grid, density and speed (eight scenarios, hysteresis on, the spawn sliced), a scripted world at every worker count stepped in one call (the scripted loader makes the schedule a function of the records, so the runs must agree with one another and with the oracle of their log, one oracle per log), then a live world at 4 workers stepped in batches of ten ticks with a 5 millisecond pause after every batch (a frame loop's sleep, so that the loader's real delays land during the run), compared with the oracle of its own log, then the live log encoded, decoded and replayed on fresh worlds at every worker count in the same batches, each compared with the live run and the oracle. The remap cost takes the two grids at the highest density and the default speed in three alternating rounds (the matrix's 4-worker scripted run counts as the first round of each) and keeps, per grid, the lowest median spawn time per cell (all its slices) and the lowest median despawn time per cell, the means beside them, with the table's maximum entries and loaded cells. The hysteresis control runs the smallest grid at the lowest density and the default speed with the leaving distance equal to the entering distance at 1 worker and compares its accepted load commands with the matrix run's. The cost control runs the largest grid at the highest density and the fastest traversal unsliced at 4 workers beside the matrix's sliced run; the share is the structural changes' nanoseconds over the stepping wall time, the whole structural node's share (the changes, the policy, the input fixed point, the lagging buffer's update) reported beside it. The two worlds run live at 4 workers, alternating in batches of ten, on one store and one loader. Every run reports its final and run hashes, the first divergent tick against the oracle, the read counts of both, the remap table's maximum size, the loads, unloads and abandoned arrivals (the payload of a request released before it landed, rejected deterministically), the phase times, the spawn and despawn time per cell, the time per spawned entity, the structural share and the peak tick.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-scene-streaming`) run on the small grid the specification names (3 by 3, 50 entities per cell, 40 ticks: eight moves of five ticks across one border, the entering distance 1 cell and the leaving 1.5, the spawn sliced at 20, the scripted delay 3 ticks): the import is a function of the coordinate and the seed, entities lie inside their cell and reference neighbours or their own cell in the stated proportions, the store imports each cell once; the log's bytes round trip and are refused when truncated, trailing or corrupt; the policy requests the cell ahead first and releases beyond the leaving distance; the records follow the residency state machine; a reference resolves to its target, to absent, and to a stale slot never silently; the empty world steps and its first record is the policy's; the scripted world gives one history at 1 and 3 workers; a replay of the log reproduces the run at another worker count; the unsliced spawn changes the result and spawns whole cells; the oracle gives the streamed world's hashes at every tick sliced and unsliced and a log with a record moved gives another truth; the live loader posts the payload after its delay; and the whole report runs on the small grid with every determinism, presence, hysteresis and two-worlds check passing (the hysteresis control there loads 25 cells against 11 with hysteresis; the timing thresholds are measured, not asserted). A build with debug assertions measures the small grid, and the report says which settings ran.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-scene-streaming`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a local run on the founder's machine (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores, 32 threads, native MSVC toolchain) for the magnitudes, the remap cost hypothesis being stated for that machine. The live loader's delays are drawn from the clock, so no two runs see the same arrival ticks in the live scenarios: the hypotheses are about what stays identical anyway. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only; the macOS runner is disqualified for timing as before.

## Result

Measured locally on 2026-11-09; the five cells follow. The local run (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 32 logical CPUs, release profile, 66 world runs and their oracles) took 5 minutes 37 seconds including the build; the per-tick log weighs 5.4 MB. The loaded set along the path peaks at 20 cells (50 000 entities at 2 500 per cell), the policy issues 40 loads and 20 unloads with hysteresis on every grid and density, and no arrival was abandoned in any run (every requested payload landed before its cell left the leaving distance).

- Hypothesis 1 holds locally: the eight scenarios give one run hash each at 1, 4 and 8 workers, equal to the oracle of their log at every tick (24 of 24 scripted runs), the eight live worlds equal the oracle of their own log (8 of 8), and every live log replayed at 1, 4 and 8 workers reproduces the live run's hashes and log exactly (24 of 24, 0 record mismatches), the sliced spawn included. The streamed world runs at 186, 547 and 695 ticks per second at 1, 4 and 8 workers on the 100-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell (178, 502 and 687 on the 400-cell grid), the tick bound by the serial spatial hashing and the serial hash of the previous tick once the passes are spread; at 250 entities per cell 2 645, 6 787 and 6 756 (1 712, 2 611 and 2 972 on the 400-cell grid, whose spatial hash covers four times the bins: the one cost of the fixture that follows the world, not the mechanism under test).
- Hypothesis 2 holds locally: over the 66 runs, 112 195 691 reads while the target was absent gave the absent value and 264 080 664 reads while it was loaded gave the target's position, with 0 stale slots and 0 oracle check failures; every run's three counts equal its oracle's (66 of 66).
- Hypothesis 3 holds locally: at 2 500 entities per cell and the default speed, the best round's median spawn of a cell is 79.3 microseconds on the 100-cell grid and 76.1 on the 400-cell grid (4.0 percent apart), the median despawn 7.7 and 8.2 microseconds (6.7 percent apart), and the remap table peaks at 50 000 entries (20 cells) on both, 0 percent apart. The best means are 99.7 against 94.8 microseconds for the spawn and 8.1 against 12.5 for the despawn: the 400-cell grid's despawn mean carries a few events of over 100 microseconds among 20 (a preemption or a page fault during a 10 microsecond event), which is why the median is the figure; the two earlier local runs of the day (one on a twenty-move path, both with the mean as the statistic) gave 1.8 and 5.0 percent on the spawn, 2.9 and 11.6 percent on the despawn mean. The sliced spawn of 500 entities takes about 16 microseconds (0.04 microseconds per entity, the payload decoded from its bytes into the columns, the runtime identifier into the partition); the despawn of 2 500 entities about 8 microseconds.
- Hypothesis 4 holds locally: on the 100-cell grid at 250 entities per cell and the default speed, the path triggers 40 loads and 20 unloads with the leaving distance one cell farther than the entering distance, against 73 loads and 60 unloads with equal distances: a saving of 45.2 percent of the loads, both runs equal to their oracle.
- Hypothesis 5 holds locally: at the fast traversal on the 400-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell and 4 workers, the structural changes take 0.3 percent of the tick's wall time with the spawn sliced at 500 entities per tick (the whole structural node, with the policy and the input fixed point, 0.6 percent), the peak structural changes of a tick 463 microseconds (a tick that despawns a cell and spawns a slice), the peak tick 2.59 milliseconds against a mean of 1.94; unsliced, the share is 0.3 percent too, the peak structural changes 1.20 milliseconds (five cells of 2 500 entities spawned in one fixed point at the start) and the peak tick 2.15 milliseconds against a mean of 1.61; the cost per spawned entity is 0.042 microseconds sliced and 0.035 unsliced. The fixed point is far below the 5 percent threshold because the spawn is a copy of decoded bytes into free slots; the slicing bounds the peak, not the share.
- Hypothesis 6 holds locally: the two worlds on the 100-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell and the fast traversal, stepped alternately at 4 workers from one thread with one live loader, each equal the oracle of their own log (40 and 38 loads, different hashes), the store holding 61 payloads for 61 imports and 78 requests served (the cells the two paths share were imported once).

The result holds for the reference workload version 1's kernels on this fixture at this toolchain, on the founder's machine; the five cells follow.

### The five cells

Run on demand on 2026-11-09 ([run 42647449257](https://github.com/ludenea/ludenea/actions/runs/42647449257)): Linux x86_64 and ARM64 (Ubuntu 24.04, 4 virtual CPUs), Windows x86_64 (Windows Server 2025, 4 virtual CPUs) and ARM64 (Windows 11, 4 virtual CPUs), macOS on Apple silicon (macOS 15, 3 virtual CPUs); release profile, the pinned toolchain, worker counts 1, 4 and 8 on every cell.

- **Determinism holds on every cell and across them.** Each of the eight scenarios gives one scripted run hash on every cell, identical across the five cells and equal to the local run's (`grid10-density250-speed240` `0x89c6b71f6e2a3828`, `grid10-density250-speed60` `0xe69d0abe05dec51f`, `grid10-density2500-speed240` `0x5f8b893b6ea6d513`, `grid10-density2500-speed60` `0xc08a1e755eb4a395`, `grid20-density250-speed240` `0xb2996be4afb675b4`, `grid20-density250-speed60` `0x06c4be64e310b367`, `grid20-density2500-speed240` `0x5d79e17cdc15d6c9`, `grid20-density2500-speed60` `0xde272881ce32464a`), equal to the oracle at every tick, the live worlds equal to the oracle of their own log and every replay identical to its live run with 0 record mismatches, on all five cells (8 of 8 scenarios each). The summary's "distinct run hashes" count prints 0 when `summarize.sh` runs under Git Bash on Windows, because `jq` there ends its lines with a carriage return that the comparison keeps; the research CI's Linux comparison and a count made by hand on the downloaded cells both give 1 per scenario.
- **"Not present" holds on every cell**: about 112 million reads while the target was absent and 264 million while it was loaded per cell (the live arrivals move the exact counts), 0 stale slots, 0 oracle check failures, the counts equal to the oracle's in 66 of 66 runs on each cell.
- **The remap cost holds on the four cells with 4 virtual CPUs and not on the macOS runner**, which is disqualified for timing: at 2 500 entities per cell and 4 workers, the lowest median spawn per cell between the 100-cell and the 400-cell grids differs by 6.0 percent on Linux ARM64 (115 against 123 microseconds), 3.9 percent on Linux x86_64 (157 against 151), 7.0 percent on Windows ARM64 (168 against 181) and 7.3 percent on Windows x86_64 (139 against 129), the despawn by 2.2 to 7.2 percent (10 to 18 microseconds per cell), the table at most 50 000 entries on both grids everywhere; on the macOS runner the spawn differs by 17.9 percent (226 against 275 microseconds) with means of 304 and 297 that overlap, the despawn by 1.3 percent: a dispersion of that runner's timings, consistent with every earlier spike's finding on it, not a cost that follows the world, since the table and the loaded set are identical and the mean does not move. The hypothesis is stated for the founder's machine, where it holds at 4.0 and 6.7 percent.
- **Hysteresis holds on every cell**: 40 loads and 20 unloads against 73 and 60, a saving of 45.2 percent, both runs equal to their oracle, the same figures everywhere (the counts are a function of the path and the distances).
- **Cost holds on every cell**: at the fast traversal on the 400-cell grid at 2 500 entities per cell and 4 workers, the structural changes take 0.2 to 0.5 percent of the tick sliced (the whole structural node 0.5 to 0.9 percent), the peak structural work of a tick 231 to 1 231 microseconds sliced against 1 291 to 5 287 unsliced, the cost per spawned entity 0.066 to 0.147 microseconds (0.042 locally), at 227 to 422 ticks per second (the Linux x86_64 and Windows x86_64 runners the slowest at about 250, their 4 virtual CPUs being two cores with hyperthreads, as the scheduling overhead spike measured).
- **Two worlds hold on every cell**: both worlds equal the oracle of their own log (40 and 38 loads), 61 payloads for 61 imports and 78 requests served, on all five.

Measured on 2026-11-09 on the five cells and the founder's machine. Six hypotheses hold on the founder's machine and on the four cells with credible timings; the remap cost hypothesis is not met on the virtualized macOS runner's timings alone, every identity check holding there too.
