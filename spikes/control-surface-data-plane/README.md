# Control surface data plane and transactions

Throwaway spike of the control surface research. Over the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) and the world of the [control surface toy](../control-surface-toy/README.md) spike, it builds what the toy left unmeasured: a shared memory ring per session handed over the local transport, carrying the full extracted state and a stand-in for a rendered frame under a lossless or a latest-wins policy; the same streams over a second socket connection; transactions of 1, 10 and 100 elements validated as a whole at the input fixed point, with a full-evaluation mode; the inverse transaction returned on request and applied as a new transaction; and a per-session command sequence whose record is the input log. It measures, instead of assuming, what the ring costs the client and the world, whether a client that stops reading reaches the world, whether a transaction ever applies in part, whether the inverse restores the state hash and at what cost, and whether a retry, a skipped sequence and an altered retry are handled as designed, with the sequential oracle's hashes at every tick.

## Layout

- `src/world.rs`: the world, a trimmed copy of the toy spike's (the reference workload on the dependency-counting tick graph, stepped by whole ticks). Two differences: inputs no longer travel inside the graph, since between two steps the world rests at the input fixed point and the surface mutates the state there in admission order; and the node that hashes a completed tick hands the state to an extraction sink.
- `src/commands.rs`: the elements of a transaction (a kick, a field set, the add and the remove of a component, a spawn, a despawn, and the restore an inverse is made of), their validation against the state as the accepted elements before them leave it, their application, the inverse, the verdicts (accepted, rejected with a named reason, not evaluated), the canonical bytes, the digests a client observes (the tick hash, the hash of the component data without the generations, the labels), and the sequential oracle. One function is shared by the world and the oracle so that both decide alike.
- `src/shared_memory.rs`: one shared memory object and its hand-over, with the platform calls declared by hand: an anonymous memory file on Linux and an unlinked POSIX shared memory object on macOS, whose descriptor travels over the Unix domain socket as ancillary data; a named file mapping section on Windows, whose name travels as text.
- `src/ring.rs`: the ring of frames inside the mapping. One writer and one reader, each through its own view; three byte positions as atomics inside the mapping (written, read, and the oldest byte still valid); a frame is a 32-byte header (world, stream, tick, length, sequence) and the payload. A lossless frame is written only when it fits; a latest-wins frame may overwrite the oldest frames, and a reader that was copying one discards it.
- `src/data_plane.rs`: the extracted state (the stepping thread serializes into a recycled buffer and swaps it in under a short lock, with a short retention standing for the history a lossless stream resumes from), the streams and their policies, the second stream's socket sink, and the one thread that copies frames, standing for the I/O executor.
- `src/surface.rs`: sessions (stepped and paced), the command sequence and the input log, the data plane's opening, the structured errors and the binary envelope.
- `src/transport.rs`: the loopback TCP socket and the Unix domain socket, a trimmed copy of the toy's, plus the descriptor's hand-over and the connection that becomes a second stream.
- `src/agent.rs`: the client's calls and its reader thread. `src/script.rs`: the transactions the scripted agent sends. `src/scenarios.rs`: the runs. `src/main.rs`: the report and the verdicts.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander system off, one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features, at 1 and 4 workers:

- **The ring as the local data plane.** Delivering the full extracted state (250 KB per tick) through the ring costs the client under a tenth of the full-state read through the socket, and the world's throughput under a lossless stream stays within 2 percent of the world stepped directly, the copy into the ring running on the thread that stands for the I/O executor. Refuted by a client cost above half the socket read's or a throughput loss above 2 percent.
- **Flow control never reaches the world.** A client that stops reading a latest-wins stream changes neither the world's hashes nor its throughput; one that stops reading a lossless stream sees it ended at the budget with the structured error and resumes from its cursor with every frame once. Refuted by any hash change under a stalled client.
- **Transactions are atomic.** Every transaction applies entirely or not at all, with the oracle's hashes, and the full-evaluation mode lists every rejection without applying anything. Refuted by one transaction applied in part.
- **The inverse is the undo base.** Applying the inverse transaction returns the state hash to its value before the original, for every kind and size, and computing the inverse costs under 10 percent of applying the transaction. A hash not restored refutes the inverse as the undo base; a cost above 10 percent makes the inverse an opt-in.
- **The command sequence is the idempotency window.** A retried sequence is answered from the input log, a sequence that skips ahead is refused, a retry with other bytes is refused by name, and the hashes equal the oracle's throughout. Refuted by a retry applied twice or a gap admitted.

The program computes each verdict from its measurements and prints it with its figure and its threshold.

## Method

Per worker count:

- **Throughput**, three repetitions interleaved, the best kept, over the time the world spends stepping: the world stepped directly in batches of ten; then a stepped session over the local transport that only steps, with no data plane, with the extraction alone (a ring and no stream), with a lossless ring, with a latest-wins ring read, with a latest-wins ring whose client stops reading at tick 300 for the rest of the run, and with a lossless second stream. Each stream set is a state stream and a frame stream (230 400 bytes per tick). The client's reader runs on its own thread through its own view of the shared memory object, as another process would, and copies every frame into a buffer it owns; its cost is the time it spends obtaining the bytes, waiting excluded.
- **The socket read**: 200 full-state reads (the same canonical bytes) through each local socket, the median kept; the ring's client cost is divided by the read on the transport the ring's session uses.
- **Stalled clients**: a latest-wins second stream whose client stalls; a lossless ring and a lossless second stream whose client stops reading at tick 300, sees the streams end, reads what the sink still holds ten ticks later and resumes each stream at the sequence after the last it received. It resumes the same way after every later end, which the script does not cause: on a contended machine the reader may not be scheduled for the budget's worth of ticks, and the stream ends again. The ends are counted per stream, and the check is on the whole run: every frame once, the client's cursor equal to the server's on every resume, the oracle's hashes. The reader hashes every state frame and the hash must equal the one the step announced for that tick.
- **Commands**: the scripted agent kicks sixteen watched entities every ten ticks (one command each) and, at fifteen boundaries, sends one kind of transaction at one size (field sets, component adds and removes, despawns, spawns, a mix of all with kicks; 1, 10 and 100 elements): the transaction with its middle element replaced by a refused one, the transaction with its first, middle and last elements replaced by refused ones under full evaluation, the valid transaction with its inverse asked for, the inverse as a new transaction, and the original again. The digests are read before and after each. At three boundaries it retries the last command, sends a sequence that skips one, and retries the last sequence with other bytes; at the end it retries its first command, older than the 300 ticks the log retains. A lossless data plane is read and verified beside it, once through the ring and once through the second stream.
- **The oracle**: the reference crate's sequential systems, given the agent's own record of what it sent and of the tick boundary each command was applied at. Its hashes are compared at every tick, its outcomes with every command's and its digests with every digest read.

Once, at 4 workers: the paced session (a host steps one tick per millisecond; the agent polls, sends its kicks, which wait in the input log for the next tick boundary, and asks for every result by retrying the sequence; its reader stalls at tick 300 and resumes by cursor). Once, in process: the inverse's cost, the same transactions applied to copies of one state with and without the inverse, 61 alternating rounds, the median per transaction.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p control-surface-data-plane`) verify the ring's wrap-around, its lossless refusal and its overwrite, that a reader racing an overwriting writer over 200 000 frames never delivers a torn frame, that two views share their bytes and a descriptor travels over a Unix domain socket, the pause, the end at the budget and the resume by cursor, two forced ends each resumed with every frame once, a transaction's atomicity, every named rejection, the inverse of every kind at every size, the sequence's four answers, a paced session's results from the log, and the whole report on the small size, where ticks are shorter than the reader's polling period and the clients of lossless streams therefore wait for their reader between two steps (a client that does not is ended at the budget, by design).

### Interpretations

- **Component add and remove.** The reference workload has a fixed set of components. The component a transaction adds or removes is a spike-local `label` (one integer per entity) kept beside the state and bound to the generation it was added to. It is outside the workload's tick hash, so it has its own digest.
- **Spawn.** The slot table has a fixed size: a spawn takes the lowest dead slot, as the workload's own spawns do, and is rejected when none is free. A transaction of spawns is therefore preceded by despawns that free its slots.
- **The inverse of a despawn** is a restore: the entity comes back at its handle with its components and its label. When a spawn of the same transaction reused the slot, the restore rolls the slot's generation back to the despawned handle's; without that, the inverse of a transaction that despawns and spawns would be refused. **The inverse of a spawn** is a plain despawn.
- **The budget.** The design under study ends a lossless stream past the session's unacknowledged budget. Here the stream ends once the world has advanced by 24 ticks while the stream waited on a full sink and the client made room for no frame; any frame the sink takes ends the pause. A budget counted on the backlog alone would end a stream again while its client catches up after a resume.
- **Latest-wins over a socket** cannot overwrite what the socket holds: it skips to the newest tick once the socket takes a frame again.
- **Stepped commands** are validated and applied on arrival, the world resting at the fixed point between two steps, and their verdicts return in the same round trip. The toy's kick is an element like the others.
- **The socket read** compared with the ring is measured here (a request, then 250 KB in the reply). The schema spike's figure for a full-state read was a decode and a hash in process, not a socket read.
- **Trimmed from the toy**: the contract's description and its discovery, the in-process endpoints and the reconnection, which the toy measured.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=control-surface-data-plane`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target. The ring's session uses the Unix domain socket on Linux and macOS and the loopback socket on Windows. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only.

## What it does not measure

A network transport; the named pipe on Windows (the loopback socket stands in, as in the toy); a real renderer behind the frame stream; a second process (the client's reader is a thread with its own view of the shared memory object, which exercises the hand-over and the ring but not two address spaces); the interference of many sessions, which the headless modes spike covered on the world side; a sampled stream policy; a change set as the state stream's content (the full state is carried at every tick).

## Result

Local run of 2026-11-09 on an AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D (16 cores, 32 threads, Windows 11, native MSVC), outside the matrix, under the cell label `local-data-plane`. The results of the five cells are to be recorded here after the research CI run. On Windows the loopback socket is the only transport, so the descriptor's hand-over and the two Unix structure layouts have not run yet.

Every hash, outcome and digest equals the oracle's in every run. Best of three repetitions, ticks per second over the time spent stepping:

| Workers | Direct | Surface only | Extraction only | Lossless ring | Latest-wins ring | Latest-wins ring, client stalled | Lossless second stream |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 931 | 922 | 908 | 906 | 907 | 912 | 897 |
| 4 | 2 895 | 2 902 | 2 857 | 2 850 | 2 842 | 2 841 | 2 791 |

- **The ring as the local data plane: fails on the throughput at one worker, holds on the client cost.** The client obtains a state frame from the ring in 5.1 to 5.4 microseconds (a frame stand-in in 3.9 to 4.1), against 588 to 632 microseconds (median) for the full-state read through the loopback socket (583 to 735 over the runs): 0.009 of it, far under a tenth. The copy into the ring costs the copying thread 5.2 to 5.3 microseconds per state frame. The world loses 1.6 percent of its throughput under a lossless ring at 4 workers and 2.7 percent at 1 worker, above the 2 percent threshold (2.1 to 3.3 percent over the last five runs of the spike; an earlier run, whose repetitions of one way were 10 percent apart, showed 11.3). The cause the measurement names is the extraction, not the ring: with a ring and no stream the loss is already 2.5 percent at 1 worker (2.0 to 3.1 over the runs) and 1.3 percent at 4 (1.0 to 1.4). The extraction serializes 250 KB row by row in 24 microseconds on a stepping thread; at one worker nothing overlaps it and it is 2.2 percent of a 1.07 millisecond tick, while at 4 workers it overlaps the next tick's passes with the hash.
- **Flow control never reaches the world: holds.** A client that stops reading a latest-wins ring at tick 300 leaves the hashes equal to the oracle's and the throughput within 0.6 percent of the reading client's (between a gain of 2.0 and a loss of 0.4 percent over the runs), while the endpoint overwrites 1 385 frames per run; a stalled latest-wins second stream leaves the hashes unchanged as well. A client that stops reading a lossless stream at tick 300 sees both streams ended with `unacknowledged_budget_exceeded` (at tick 340 through the ring, 330 through the second stream), reads what the sink still holds, resumes at its cursors (tick 308 or 309 through the ring, 303 through the second stream, equal to the server's own cursors) and receives every frame of the 1 000 ticks once, in order, each state frame hashing to the tick's announced hash, at 1 and 4 workers and under the paced host (518 host ticks per second; ended at tick 332, resumed at 342). Each stream ended once in every run on this machine.
- **The second stream is slower than the stepped world at 4 workers.** The client reads a state frame from the loopback socket in 383 to 420 microseconds (325 to 605 over the runs). A stepped client that waits for its reader between two steps sustains 761 to 997 ticks per second of its own time; one that does not wait outruns the stream, which ends at its budget after 126 state frames at 4 workers (35 to 130 over the runs), the world's hashes and throughput unchanged. The agent's commands run with a lossless second stream give the same hashes and every frame once.
- **Transactions are atomic: holds.** 60 of 60 kinds and sizes (15 per run, two data planes, two worker counts): the valid transaction applies whole; with one refused element the elements before it are accepted, it is rejected with `stale_handle` and the rest are not evaluated (0, 5 and 50 accepted at sizes 1, 10 and 100), and the digests do not move; under full evaluation the 1 or 3 planted refusals are all listed, none left unevaluated, and the digests do not move. 1 679 commands per run, every outcome and each of the 91 digests equal to the oracle's.
- **The inverse is the undo base: fails as stated, on both counts.** The inverse applies in 60 of 60 cases and restores the component data and the labels in 60 of 60; it restores the full tick hash in 48 of 60. The 12 others are the transactions of spawns alone: a spawn undone by a despawn leaves the slot dead with the generation the spawn gave it, and the tick hash covers the generation of dead slots. In the mixed transactions every spawn reused a slot the same transaction had despawned, the restore rolled the generation back (1 restore at size 10, 15 at size 100) and the full hash is restored. The original applies again after the inverse in 60 of 60. Computing the inverse costs 4 to 148 percent of applying the transaction in process, at or above 10 percent for 12 of 15 kinds and sizes: applying is 31 nanoseconds for one field set and 0.9 microseconds for a mixed transaction of 100, and the inverse adds 18 nanoseconds and 0.4 microseconds to them. A single kick, undone by two field sets, costs 147 percent in every run; ten field sets cost 38 percent in this run and 220 percent in two others, the median falling on either side of a step in the time of the inverse's buffer. Which kinds stay under 10 percent varies between runs: here a single despawn and the spawns of 10 and 100.
- **The command sequence is the idempotency window: holds.** In every run the three retries are answered from the log with the first answer, the three sequences that skip one are refused with `sequence_gap` naming the expected sequence, the three retries with other bytes are refused with `altered_retry`, none of them moves the digests, and the retry of the first command, older than the retained log, is refused with `outside_retention`. The log holds each command once (1 679 sent, 1 679 logged, 111 bytes per tick). Under the paced host the 1 568 commands wait in the log for the next tick boundary and each result is answered from the log by a retry of its sequence.

The raw measurements and the environment of the run are in `out/` after `ci/run-spike.sh control-surface-data-plane <cell>`. The result holds for the reference workload version 1 at this toolchain on this machine.
