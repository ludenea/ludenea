# Reference workload scheduling overhead

Throwaway spike of the multithreading research. It runs the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) under four schedulers and measures, instead of assuming, what each scheduler construct costs and whether a dependency-counting task graph that overlaps the serial phases of a tick with the next tick's parallel work keeps the hashes of the sequential reference on every cell of the desktop boundary and at every worker count.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander system off, compiled by one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features:

1. A dependency-counting task graph over double-buffered state, with chunked parallel passes and the hash of a tick running while the next tick's spatial hashing, repulsion and motion proceed, reproduces the run hash of the sequential reference at 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers on every cell, and at 10 workers on a 10-core machine: the deterministic subset is identical at both core counts.
2. Its throughput at 4 workers (the runners) and at 10 workers (the local machine) exceeds the barrier pool's, by overlapping the serial phases, with the ceiling given by Amdahl's law on the measured serial share of the barrier pool (on the ARM64 Linux cell of the worker sweep: 663 ms per 1 000 ticks, of which 323 serial).
3. Writing disjoint mutable chunk slices into the state removes the copy back phase of the worker sweep's pool (54 to 126 ms per 1 000 ticks at 4 workers) at no cost in determinism.
4. The scheduling overhead per task, measured on the same graph with empty kernels, is below 5 percent of the tick at the chunk size of 256, and the chunk size curve (64, 128, 256, 512, 1 024) has a knee.
5. A central atomic counter per node and per-worker ranges with work stealing give the same throughput on this regular workload at 4 and 10 workers, so that work stealing is not needed for frame-bound chunked work.
6. A bounded spin followed by parking scales on the virtualized macOS runner while an unbounded spin does not, reproducing on an engine-owned pool what the Box2D worker sweep showed on the library's spinning solver.
7. A panic injected in one chunk fails the tick without deadlocking the pool, and the run reports the failing node, chunk and tick.

## Method

Four schedulers run the same systems (spatial hashing, repulsion, motion as wander, integration, boundary and energy, structural changes, hash):

- `barrier-copy-back`: the pool of the [worker sweep spike](../workload-worker-sweep/README.md), re-implemented here: a persistent set of threads (the main thread counts as one worker) synchronized by a barrier at both ends of each parallel pass; chunks of 256 slots claimed through one atomic counter; per-chunk output buffers under a lock, copied back into the state by the main thread between passes; spatial hashing, structural changes and the hash on the main thread. It reproduces the baseline of the worker sweep.
- `barrier-in-place`: the same pool, each chunk writing its own slots of the position and velocity columns in place through disjoint mutable slices (the one `unsafe` construct of the spike), so that the output buffers and the copy back disappear.
- `graph-counter`: a dependency-counting task graph per tick over two copies of the state. The nodes are the spatial hashing (one chunk), the repulsion pass and the motion pass (one chunk per slice of the population), the structural changes (one chunk) and the hash (one chunk). A node is armed when its predecessors complete: the passes read the buffer holding the state after the previous tick and write the other buffer; the structural changes wait for the motion pass of their tick and for the hash of the previous tick, run alone, bring the written buffer's liveness, generations, masses and stream state up to date (only the slots the previous structural changes touched differ between the two buffers), reduce the energy in the specified order, apply the kills and spawns and swap the buffers; the hash of the finished tick is then armed together with the next tick's spatial hashing, so that it runs on one worker while the other workers start the next tick. The chunks of an armed node are claimed from one atomic counter; the arming worker takes the single chunk of a serial node itself. A panic in a task is caught at the task boundary, recorded with its tick, node, chunk and worker, the task still counts as finished, and the run ends at the first hash that completes after the failure.
- `graph-stealing`: the same graph, the chunks of an armed node pre-partitioned into one contiguous range per worker; a worker claims from the front of its own range and, when it is empty, steals one chunk at a time from the back of another worker's range, as a work-stealing deque does. (A first version stole the back half of a range and kept it as the thief's own range; the store that publishes the kept half races with the arming of the node when a worker holding a stale view of the ready nodes steals while the per-worker ranges are being written, and the chunks it took were lost: the empty-kernel runs, where every task is instant, deadlocked. The self-checks now include a watchdog run of the empty graph.)

Waiting policies of the graph when no chunk is claimable: `park` (park the thread on the operating system at once, woken by the worker that arms new work), `spin-then-park` (spin for a bound, 50 microseconds by default, then park), `spin` (spin until work appears, never park).

Runs, all on the default size for 1 000 ticks with the wander system off, each after a warm-up instance of 10 ticks: the sequential reference; the four schedulers with `spin-then-park` at 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers plus the machine's available parallelism when it is not in that list (10 on the local machine, 3 on the macOS runner); the counter graph with `park` and with `spin` at the same worker counts; a chunk size sweep (64, 128, 512 and 1 024 slots, 256 being the default) of the in-place pool and the counter graph at 4 workers and, locally, at 10; a spin bound sweep (5, 20, 200 and 1 000 microseconds) of the counter graph at the same worker counts; an empty-kernel run of 10 000 ticks of the in-place pool, the counter graph and the stealing graph at every worker count, which measures the scheduler alone and reports microseconds per tick and per task; one run of the counter graph on the small size with a panic injected in the second chunk of the repulsion pass at tick 3. Chunks smaller than an energy group of 256 slots leave one energy per slot that the fixed point sums in the specified group order; larger chunks sum whole groups, so that every chunk size reproduces the specified order of additions.

Every run reports the run hash, the first tick at which it diverges from the sequential run of the same cell, ticks per second, the utilization (busy time of every task over the workers' wall time, from per-task timestamps on every worker), the busy time per node, the time spent waiting, the number of parks and of steals. For the barrier pools `phase_ms` is the main thread's wall time per phase, as in the worker sweep; for the graphs it is the busy time per node summed over the workers.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-scheduling-overhead`) verify on the small size that every scheduler reproduces the sequential tick hashes at 1, 2, 3 and 8 workers, under every waiting policy and every chunk size, with the wander system off and on; that the stealing ranges split as intended; that the empty graph executes the expected number of tasks; and that a panic injected in a pass, in the hash or in the structural changes is reported with its tick, node and chunk, the hashes before the failed tick match the reference, and the pool terminates.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-scheduling-overhead`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a build with `-f profile=dev` on Linux x86_64, and a local run on an Apple M5 (10 cores, 4 performance and 6 efficiency, macOS 26) outside the matrix for the 10-worker runs. The runners have 4 virtual CPUs (3 on macOS), so the 8-worker runs oversubscribe them on purpose. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only, and the macOS runner's timings are read for the waiting policy question only.

- Hypothesis 1 holds: every parallel run reproduces the sequential run hash `0x9f50cc4763ae733c` at every tick, on the five cells (36 of 36 parallel runs per cell, 42 of 42 on macOS with its extra worker count), on the unoptimized build (36 of 36) and on the local machine at 1 to 10 workers (54 of 54), under every scheduler, waiting policy and chunk size: one distinct run hash across everything. The deterministic subset is identical at 4 and 10 cores.
- Hypothesis 2 holds: the graph scheduler reaches 1.31 to 1.55 times the throughput of the in-place barrier pool at 4 workers on the four Linux and Windows cells (1.42 on macOS at 3 workers), 1.75 at 4 workers and 1.9 at 8 and 10 workers on the local machine, with a utilization (busy time of every task over the workers' wall time) of 0.80 on the x86_64 cells and 0.89 to 0.90 on the ARM64 cells against 0.56 to 0.59 for the barrier pools. The gain is the hash overlapped: the hash costs 224 ms per 1 000 ticks on the ARM64 cells and 313 ms on the x86_64 cells, and the graph reaches 95 to 107 percent of the throughput the in-place pool would have if its hash cost nothing (on the Neoverse N2 cell, 605 ms per 1 000 ticks minus 224 gives a ceiling of 2 625 ticks per second; the graph does 2 561). The structural changes stay a serial node of 8 to 14 ms per 1 000 ticks, and the spatial hashing stays on the critical path.
- Hypothesis 3 holds: the per-chunk output buffers and their copy back cost 62 to 80 ms per 1 000 ticks at 4 workers on the five cells (8 to 13 percent of the copy-back pool's tick) and 34 ms on the local machine; writing disjoint chunk slices in place removes the phase and raises the barrier pool's throughput by 5 to 16 percent on the cells and not at all on the local machine, where the in-place passes are slower by about the same amount, with identical hashes everywhere.
- Hypothesis 4 holds on five of the six machines, not on the Windows ARM64 cell. On the empty kernel at 4 workers, the graph's tick costs 5.3 to 7.7 microseconds for 83 tasks (0.06 to 0.09 microseconds per task) on the Linux, Windows x86_64, macOS and local machines, 0.6 to 2 percent of the real tick at 4 workers; on the Windows ARM64 cell it costs 26 microseconds (0.31 per task), 6.5 percent of the tick, where the barrier pool's empty tick costs 20 microseconds for its 80 chunk timestamps alone: the per-task timestamps of the instrument, not the scheduler, are the suspect on that cell. The barrier pool's empty tick costs 20 to 146 microseconds at 4 workers and 66 to 408 at 8, the graph's 5 to 26 at both: the graph's synchronization cost is 4 to 26 times lower and insensitive to oversubscription. The chunk size curve has no knee between 64 and 512 slots (within 2 percent at 4 workers on every cell and on the local machine, barrier and graph alike); 1 024 slots, ten chunks for four workers, cost 4 to 7 percent of load imbalance on the x86_64 cells and the local machine.
- Hypothesis 5 holds on the four Linux and Windows cells (2 561 against 2 571, 1 134 against 1 131, 2 521 against 2 551, 1 204 against 1 209 ticks per second at 4 workers: equal within noise) and the stealing variant is faster where the shared counter is contended on fewer or slower cores: 16 percent at 3 workers on the virtualized macOS runner (1 534 against 1 319), 6 percent at 4 workers on the local machine (3 506 against 3 310), equal at 8 and 10. A worker steals 7 to 8 chunks per tick at 4 workers on the ARM64 cells, 3 to 5 on the x86_64 cells, 12 on macOS. One-chunk stealing is never slower than the counter.
- Hypothesis 6 holds. The unbounded spin collapses as soon as workers exceed virtual CPUs: on the macOS cell (3 vCPUs) from 1 371 ticks per second at 3 workers to 642 at 4 and 243 at 8, and on the 4-vCPU cells at 8 workers to 706, 428, 570 and 307 ticks per second (a quarter to a third of `spin-then-park`, which keeps 93 to 95 percent of its 4-worker throughput at 8). At the exact core count the unbounded spin is 2 to 7 percent slower than `spin-then-park`, never faster. Parking at once costs 2 to 7 percent against `spin-then-park` at 4 workers on the Linux and Windows cells, 14 percent at 3 workers on macOS and 6 percent at 4 workers on the local machine. The spin bound sweep at 4 workers saturates at 20 to 50 microseconds on the cells that gain from spinning (Neoverse N2: 2 427, 2 499, 2 561, 2 557, 2 567 ticks per second at 5, 20, 50, 200 and 1 000 microseconds, with the parks falling from 5 821 to 0), is flat on the x86_64 cells, and a long bound costs when the machine is shared or heterogeneous (macOS at 4 workers on 3 vCPUs: 1 461 at 20 microseconds against 1 009 at 1 000; the local machine at 10 workers: 3 810 at 5 against 3 281 at 1 000).
- Hypothesis 7 holds on every cell and on the unoptimized build: the panic injected in the second chunk of the repulsion pass at tick 3 is caught, reported with its tick, node, chunk and worker, the pool terminates, the hashes of ticks 0 to 2 match the reference, and the run ends at the first hash completing after the failure (the hash of tick 3 on the five cells and the local machine, the hash of tick 2 on the unoptimized build, which was still running when the failure struck).
- Two facts about the cells, read from the busy times: at 4 workers the repulsion pass costs 1.41 to 1.43 times more processor time than at 1 worker on the two x86_64 cells (2 063 against 1 465 ms per 1 000 ticks on Linux) and 1.03 times more on the ARM64 cells, which confirms that the x86_64 runners' 4 virtual CPUs are two cores with hyperthreads and the ARM64 runners' are four cores; and the macOS runner's timings vary by up to 25 percent between runs of the same configuration, so its numbers above serve the waiting policy question only.

The raw measurements and the environment of each cell are the artifacts of the runs. The result holds for the reference workload version 1 at this toolchain and on these images.
