# Late asset arrival

Throwaway spike of the assets research. It runs the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) with one piece of domain data the simulation depends on, the repulsion parameters table, delivered by a loader thread after a real delay that differs between runs, and measures, instead of assuming, what the fence between asset arrival and the deterministic domain gives and costs: whether a world that requires the asset waits for it and then gives the reference hashes at every worker count whatever the delay; whether a world that reads a declared default until the arrival takes effect reproduces its run from the recorded input log at every worker count, the arrival tick being an input; what happens when the fence is removed and the systems read the payload the moment it lands; whether a hot reload of the table at a scripted tick is deterministic; what the fence costs per tick; and what a failed transition does.

## Layout

- `src/world.rs`: a trimmed copy of the [runtime skeleton](../runtime-skeleton/README.md) spike's core (the reference workload stepped by whole ticks on the dependency-counting tick graph; no snapshot, no injection) with the asset machinery. The asset is identified by a handle and by the content hash of its canonical bytes (strength and radius, little-endian, FNV-1a). The world holds its availability state (unavailable, available at a content hash, failed), the current table (the domain-resident asset data) and the input log. A loader posts arrivals to the arrival queue; the input fixed point, which runs before every tick (in the structural node of the previous tick, which runs alone, or on the calling thread between steps), drains the queue, sorts the arrivals by request identifier, appends the scripted arrivals due at that tick, applies each as a transition (a payload for the known asset whose bytes decode and hash to the announced content hash makes the asset available; anything else makes it failed, with the default table) and records every transition in the log with the tick it takes effect at. The repulsion pass reads the table as domain state: the specification's kernel copied with the strength and the radius as parameters, the same operations in the same order, so that version 1 of the table (64, 8) gives the reference's bits; while the asset is unavailable the pass reads the declared default (0, 8), no repulsion. A world may require the asset: it then refuses to step until the asset is available (the loading gate) and records the gate's opening tick. The control mode removes the fence: the pass reads a shared word the loader writes directly, whenever it lands, and the world records the tick during which a chunk first read the new value. The plain mode is the skeleton's kernel with the constants and no asset at all, the cost baseline. The file also holds the sequential oracle: the reference implementation's systems with the table switched at the ticks a schedule names, so that any arrival tick has its sequential truth.
- `src/main.rs`: the scenarios and the report. Every world's tick hashes are compared with the sequential reference or with the oracle of its own schedule; replays are built from the recorded log's bytes, decoded, with the payloads the host knows by content hash.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander system off, compiled by one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features, worker counts 1, 2, 4 and 8 (and the machine's CPU count when different):

1. The loading gate: a world that requires the table, with the loader landing after a real delay (drawn from the clock between 20 and 300 ms, and explicitly 0 and 500 ms), refuses to step while the asset is unavailable, opens its gate before tick 1 once the arrival is applied, and then gives the reference hashes at every worker count whatever the delay; the gate's cost is the real delay and nothing more.
2. The declared default: a world that does not require the table steps at once in batches of ten ticks and reads the default until the tick the arrival takes effect at, a tick the real delay decides; that tick is an input recorded in the log, so that a fresh world fed the recorded log reproduces the live run at every worker count, and the sequential oracle with the table switched at that tick agrees with both at every tick. Two runs with different delays land at different ticks and give different run hashes (different inputs, not a divergence), each reproduced by its own replay.
3. The fence removed: when the repulsion pass reads the table the moment the loader stores it, runs diverge at the tick during which the payload landed, from one another and from the oracle switched at that tick, because the moment of the read inside the tick differs between runs and between chunks.
4. The fence costs nothing measurable: one availability read per chunk per tick and the queue drained at every fixed point leave the fenced world's ticks per second inside the noise of the plain world's, at 1 and 4 workers.
5. A hot reload is an availability transition like any other: version 2 of the table (48, 8), scripted at the fixed point of tick 500, gives identical hashes across worker counts, equal to the oracle switched at tick 500, and the recorded log replays it.
6. Failed transitions are deterministic: an unknown asset, a payload of the wrong length and a payload whose bytes do not hash to the announced content hash each make the asset failed at the fixed point, with the reason in the log, and the world keeps stepping with the default and equals the oracle switched at the tick of the good payload that follows.

## Method

The host computes the sequential reference and the oracle with version 1 at tick 1 (they must agree: the parameterized kernel is the specification's). Gate: for every worker count, three worlds (a clock delay, 0 ms, 500 ms), each with a loader thread that sleeps the delay then posts the arrival; the host tries one step while loading (expected refused), polls the gate every 200 microseconds, then steps 1 000 ticks in one call. Default and replay: two worlds at 4 workers, each stepped in batches of ten while its loader sleeps a clock delay; the recorded log is encoded, decoded and replayed on fresh worlds at every worker count; the oracle is switched at the arrival tick. Fence removed: at 1 and 4 workers, two worlds each, the loader writing the shared word after a clock delay; each run is compared with the oracle switched at the tick during which a chunk first read the new value, with the oracle switched one tick later, and the two runs with each other. Hot reload: for every worker count, a gated world whose loader delivers version 1 after a clock delay and whose script applies version 2 at tick 500, compared with the oracle switched at 1 and 500 and replayed from its log at the same worker count. Cost: at 1 and 4 workers, a plain world and a fenced world (version 1 scripted at tick 1, no loader), each after a 10-tick warm-up world, 1 000 ticks each. Failed transitions: one world at 4 workers with the three bad arrivals scripted at ticks 125, 250 and 375 and the good one at 500.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-asset-arrival`) verify on the small size over 40 ticks that the parameterized kernel with version 1 gives the reference kernel's bits slot by slot and that the oracle with version 1 at tick 1 is the reference run; that the gate refuses to step, opens on the arrival and gives the reference hashes at 1, 2 and 3 workers; that the default mode equals the oracle of its arrival tick and replays from its encoded log at another worker count; that two scripted arrival ticks give two run hashes, each equal to its oracle; that the hot reload at a scripted tick equals the oracle at 1 and 3 workers and replays; that an unknown asset, a short payload and a wrong content hash are logged as failed with their reasons while the world keeps stepping with the default; that the control reads the shared word; that the plain mode is the skeleton; that the log's bytes round trip and are refused with a reason when truncated, trailing or corrupt; and that the whole report runs on the small size with every check passing.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-asset-arrival`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a local run on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26) outside the matrix. The real delays are drawn from the clock, so no two runs of the spike see the same arrival ticks in the default and fence-removed scenarios: the hypotheses are about what stays identical anyway. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only.

## Result

Measured on 2026-11-08 by the research CI on the five cells (run 42518336102, the raw measurements and the environment of each cell in its artifacts), after a local validation run on 2026-11-03 on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26, worker counts 1, 2, 4, 8 and 10, taken while other builds ran). On every cell the oracle with version 1 at tick 1 equals the sequential reference (run hash `0x9f50cc4763ae733c`), and the two tables have one content hash each across the cells (version 1 `0x6ff0a205887272cc`, version 2 `0x3e1899825d2fa90c`).

- Hypothesis 1 holds on the five cells: the 63 gated worlds (12 per cell, 15 on the 3-CPU macOS runner: delays of 0 and 500 ms and clock delays of 21 to 300 ms at every worker count) all refused the step attempted while loading, opened their gate before tick 1 and gave the reference hashes (one distinct run hash across the 63 runs and the 15 local ones). The gate wait is the loader's delay plus 0.1 to 1.1 ms of polling and thread scheduling on the Linux and Windows cells; on the macOS runner the loader's sleep itself overshoots the requested delay by up to 74 ms (500 ms requested, 574 measured), the runner's sleep behavior seen by the skeleton spike, and the gate adds 0.2 to 1.5 ms to it.
- Hypothesis 2 holds on the five cells: the ten default worlds (two per cell at 4 workers) saw the arrival take effect at ticks 35 to 440 (delays of 24 to 298 ms), each differing from the reference from tick 1 and equal to the oracle switched at its own tick at every tick; the recorded log (33 bytes, one transition) replayed each run exactly at every worker count (4 of 4 on the 4-CPU cells, 5 of 5 on macOS); the two runs of every cell land at different ticks and have different run hashes.
- Hypothesis 3 holds on the five cells: with the fence removed, the two runs of a worker count differ from each other on every cell (10 of 10 pairs, diverging at the earlier of their two landing ticks), and no run matches the oracle switched one tick after the landing. At 1 worker all 10 runs diverge from the oracle switched at the tick during which a chunk first read the new table, at that very tick; at 4 workers 7 of 10 runs happen to equal that oracle (the write landed before any chunk of the tick read the table) and 3 diverge at that tick. The control's point, measured: the outcome depends on where the write lands inside the tick, and only the fence makes it a function of the inputs.
- Hypothesis 4 does not hold as stated: on every cell the fenced world runs at 0.94 to 0.96 of the plain world at 1 and 4 workers (best of three alternating rounds; the macOS runner's 1.10 at 4 workers is its variance), a measurable 4 to 6 percent where the hypothesis expected nothing. The spike cannot attribute it: the fenced mode differs from the plain mode by the availability read per chunk, the queue drained at every fixed point, and the repulsion kernel reading its strength and radius from the table where the plain kernel inlines the constants, and the kernel is the workload's hottest pass, so the lost constant folding is the likelier cost and the fence proper is bounded above by these 4 to 6 percent. A variant with a parameterized kernel and no asset would separate the two; it was not run.
- Hypothesis 5 holds on the five cells: version 2 applied at the fixed point of tick 500 gives one run hash (`0x0907ded76645fffc`, the local value) at every worker count on every cell, equal to the oracle switched at 1 and 500, and the recorded log (58 bytes, two transitions) replays it at every worker count (21 of 21 runs).
- Hypothesis 6 holds on the five cells: the unknown asset, the 5-byte payload and the wrong content hash are logged as failed at ticks 125, 250 and 375 with their reasons (the asset identifier, the lengths, the two hashes), the asset stays failed until the good payload at tick 500, and the world's hashes equal the oracle switched at tick 500.

The result holds for the reference workload version 1 at this toolchain, on these runner images and the founder's machine.
