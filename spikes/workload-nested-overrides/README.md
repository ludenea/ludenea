# Nested scene instances and overrides

Throwaway spike of the scenes and levels research. On the fixture of the [scene streaming](../workload-scene-streaming/README.md) spike it nests scenes three deep (a level nests buildings, a building nests rooms and a vault), puts the five kinds of override (a field value, a component added, a component removed, an entity added, an entity removed) on every list with targets named by a path of local identifiers and outer lists applied after inner ones, and measures, instead of assuming, whether the worlds spawned from them give one history at every worker count equal to a sequential oracle's; whether applying the overrides to the payload before the spawn gives the same hashes as applying them in the stated order after a spawn; whether the import refuses planted conflicts by name; whether a family is spawned at one structural fixed point whatever its depth, required or optional; whether a child at another content hash than the one its parent's payload names fails instead of spawning; and whether every entity keeps its full reference, overridden or not.

## Layout

- `src/scene.rs`: the fixture's schema (six components in the order of their stable names: instancing, label, mass, position, reference, velocity, each with its fields in declaration order), an entity prototype (a 64-bit local identifier, its parent's, its components), the five kinds of override with a target path, the canonical payload (the prototypes sorted by local identifier, then per instancing entity the child scene, the content hash of the child payload the list was validated against and the list in its canonical order, serialized to bytes with an FNV-1a content hash and read back from them), the import as a function of a source and of the payloads it nests, with its refusals by name and position, the ordered application of a list to a payload, the payload store (every version, the current one per scene, and the scenes each nests to any depth, recorded by the import) and the store of payloads prepared outside the tick, keyed by the child payload's content hash and the hash of the lists that apply to it.
- `src/generate.rs`: the generated content, a function of a seed so that no data file is checked in. A set is four scenes: a room and a vault (leaves), a building that nests rooms and one vault, a level that nests buildings. Local identifiers are drawn at random in the authored range. A building's list on a room, and a level's list on a building, hold every kind on entities of the child; a level's list also reaches the rooms and the vault of its buildings through a path of two local identifiers, with every kind again and with items placed on what the building's own list did (a value on a field it set, the removal of an entity it changed and of one it added, a value on another it added, the removal of a component it added, the return of one it removed); the second building of a level loses its vault by the removal of its instancing entity. One entity in five holds a reference relative to its instance (to its own instance, the one above, a sibling or a nested one), a quarter of them to identifiers that only an override adds. The planted conflicts are here too.
- `src/state.rs`: the entity columns, the two per-tick hashes (the **state hash**, slot by slot in the order of the runtime identifiers, with every slot's generation; the **content hash**, instance by instance in the canonical order of their identities and member by member in the order of their local identifiers, where no runtime identifier enters), an instance identity (a root key then the local identifier of the instancing entity at each level), the resolution of a full reference through the table of its instance, the five kinds as structural and field operations on a world, and the kernels (the workload's spatial hashing, repulsion and motion; an entity moves when it has a position, a velocity and a mass).
- `src/world.rs`: a trimmed copy of the scene streaming spike's world (whole ticks on the dependency-counting tick graph, double buffered, the hash of a tick overlapping the next tick's passes) with the grid of cells replaced by instance records (wanted and actual residency, the required flag, the content hash the parent names, the lists inherited from the scenes above). The input fixed point applies the scripted commands and the arrivals and decides the loading gate; the structural node unloads (nested instances first) and spawns in the canonical order of the identities, a parent and every nested instance whose payload is available in the same batch. The overrides of an instance are applied in one of three places: **to the payload, prepared before the run**; **in the stated order after a spawn into a staging area**, whose members then enter the world in the order of their local identifiers; **in the stated order after a spawn into the world itself**, as operations on its entities.
- `src/oracle.rs`: the sequential oracle, the same log of commands and arrivals stepped by a plain loop over one buffer, with the records, the unloads, the family spawn and the slot allocation written again, and the overrides computed by another formulation than the ordered application: for every entity, component and field, the outermost list that speaks decides. It allocates either as a spawn of the overridden members does, or as the ordered application in the world does.
- `src/main.rs` and `src/report.rs`: the script, the checks, the report as one JSON document on standard output, a short summary on standard error, and the full log written to `out/<cell>/workload-nested-overrides-ticks.log` (per run and tick the two hashes, then the input log, every spawn, despawn and failure, and what the import answered to every planted conflict).

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload's kernels on families of nested instances (rooms of 150 and of 600 entities, a level family of 20 instances and 2 519 or 10 109 entities), ten scripted loads over 72 ticks (levels, a building and a room as top-level instances, required and optional, on scenes already loaded and on scenes that arrive after the command, two unloads and reloads), compiled by the pinned Rust toolchain at default target features, at 1, 4 and 8 workers:

1. **Determinism.** By each of the three places of application, the two per-tick hashes are identical at 1, 4 and 8 workers and across the five cells, and equal to the oracle's at every tick; the imported payloads are byte-identical across the cells. Refuted by a hash that differs across worker counts, cells or from the oracle.
2. **The two paths of application.** Applying the overrides to the payload before the spawn gives the same hashes at every tick as applying them in the stated order (entities removed, entities added, components removed, components added, field values; within a kind by the target's path, the component's stable name, the field's declaration order; inner lists before outer ones) after a spawn. Refuted by one differing hash between the paths. The ordered application is measured in two forms, in a staging area and in the world itself, because they do not allocate alike.
3. **Conflicts are refused by name.** The import refuses, naming the conflict and its position: a value on an entity the same list removes, two values for one field, a component both added and removed, an added entity under an identifier the child holds (the child as the inner lists left it); each planted in the building's list on a room, in the level's list on a building and in the level's list on a room through a path. A validated list gives one result in every order of the five kinds. Refuted by one planted conflict accepted, or by an order that changes a result.
4. **Families.** A family whose payloads are available is spawned at the structural fixed point of its command's tick, whole, whatever its depth (one, two and three), required or optional; a required family on scenes not yet loaded holds the world at its tick until every payload has arrived and appears whole; an optional one appears as its payloads arrive, a parent never after its child. Refuted by a family in one batch that appears over two ticks, a tick completed with a required instance missing, or a child before its parent.
5. **Another content hash fails.** A child whose available payload is at another content hash than the one its parent's payload names is not spawned: its record fails, with the reason, and the world steps. Refuted by one such child spawned.
6. **Identity.** Every entity of every loaded instance is addressed by its full reference (the instance identity and the local identifier) and resolves to itself, whether an override changed it or not; beside the same world without any override, every full reference that differs is the doing of an override that removes or adds an entity. Refuted by one entity whose identity changed.

## Method

The binary runs, for each size, the import then the worlds. **The import**: four sets of scenes are generated and imported, the nested scenes first (each list validated against the schema, against itself, then against the child as the scenes below hold it); the room of the last set is then imported again with one value changed, under its unchanged building. The conflicts are planted in a store of their own, one source per conflict, and the answer of the import recorded; a child is also changed so that the target of its parent's override is gone. Every instance of a level family then has its lists applied to its payload in the 120 orders of the five kinds, forward and backward, against the stated order. **The worlds**: the same script by each place of application at every worker count; the prepared payloads are computed before the first world, and a spawn that finds none is counted. One oracle per allocation steps the log of the first run. Each run reports its two run hashes, its reads (while the target is not present, while it is, stale slots), the instances spawned and failed, the polls the gate held, the ticks completed with a required instance missing. The three places are compared on both hashes at every tick and on a digest of the members of every spawned instance by local identifier. At tick 28 and at the end, every entity the sources state (the payload's identifiers, minus what each list removes, plus what it adds) is resolved by its full reference in the first run of each place. A control world runs the same script on the same scenes with every list empty, and the full references of the two worlds are compared at the end.

What the spike does not measure: the text syntax of a source (a source is a structure here); lists sent by command to a live world; the option to make a child appear with its parent; a budget that spreads optional spawns over ticks; the rules of the hierarchy (the parent is a field, and no generated override removes an entity that parents another); an override of the instancing component, which the import refuses; a scene that inherits from a base scene.

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-nested-overrides`) run on a small case (rooms of 40 entities, a level of two buildings of two rooms and a vault, 1 and 3 workers): a payload reads back from its bytes and a damaged one does not; the conflicts of one list are refused by name whatever the order written; an outer list is validated against the child as the inner list left it; a validated list gives one result in every order of kinds; the world operations follow a list; the oracle's resolution gives what the ordered application gives; a set is a function of its seed and holds the five kinds on every list; every planted conflict is refused by its name; and the whole report passes every check on the small case, the state hash of the ordered application in the world excepted, which the check expects to differ from the first spawn. A build with debug assertions measures the small case, and the report says which settings ran.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-nested-overrides`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a local run on the founder's machine (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores, 32 threads, native MSVC toolchain). No measurement here is a timing hypothesis; the timings reported are orders of magnitude, and those of hosted runners compare per processor only.

## Result

Measured locally on 2026-11-11; the five cells follow. The local run (Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 32 logical CPUs, release profile; 18 world runs, 2 control worlds and 4 oracles) took 16 seconds; the log weighs 200 KB. Per size, 16 payloads and 32 lists are imported, holding 2 204 overrides at the standard size (492 entities removed, 368 added, 428 components removed, 428 added, 488 field values; 1 560 of them through a path, 360 on what an inner list did) and 5 884 at the dense one; a world peaks at 10 521 and 42 081 slots, spawns 127 instances and 15 559 or 62 299 entities, and despawns 7 557 or 30 327.

- **Hypothesis 1 holds locally.** Each place of application gives one pair of run hashes at 1, 4 and 8 workers, equal to its oracle at every tick (18 of 18 runs), with the same reads as the oracle (19 478 while the target is not present and 117 441 while it is per run at the standard size, 69 933 and 444 188 at the dense one), 0 stale slots and 0 oracle check failures. The world runs at 500, 1 185 and 1 188 ticks per second at 1, 4 and 8 workers at the standard size, 61, 217 and 296 at the dense one.
- **Hypothesis 2 holds for the ordered application in a staging area and is refuted, as stated, for the ordered application in the world.** The payload prepared outside the tick and the ordered application in a staging area give the same state hash and the same content hash at every tick, at both sizes (state run hash `0xca1ac63d69a59106` and content run hash `0x618ca42248275edb` at the standard size, `0xbb611b3825cfeb5e` and `0x5e1072cb1f8d7c49` at the dense one); the 57 prepared payloads serve the 127 instances of the script and no spawn found its payload unprepared. The ordered application in the world spawns the same content, every one of the 127 spawned instances having the same digest of its members by local identifier, and gives **another state hash from the first spawn on** (tick 1; run hashes `0x4d4717af6755d406` and `0x6a45f0067974a254`): it allocates a slot to an entity it then removes and gives the freed slot, at its next generation, to an entity it adds, where a spawn of the overridden members allocates them in the order of their local identifiers. The oracle that computes the content by resolution and allocates as the ordered application in the world does equals that world at every tick, so the difference is the allocation and nothing else. From the next tick the content hash differs too (tick 2), because the fixture's kernels sum neighbours in slot order: a property of the workload, not of the overrides. The same content under other runtime identifiers is another world for every hash that covers them.
- **Hypothesis 3 holds locally.** The 13 named conflicts (four on each of the three places, and an added entity under an identifier the inner list added) are refused by name at both sizes, as are the 23 others planted (a component on a removed entity, a target the child does not hold or that the inner list removed, a component its target lacks or already holds, an added entity in the run-time range, an entity added under a removed parent, an override into a nested instance the same list removes, a local identifier used twice, a child changed so that its parent's target is gone, a nested payload at another hash): 36 of 36, none accepted. The 4 800 applications of a level family's lists in other orders of the five kinds all give the result of the stated order.
- **Hypothesis 4 holds locally.** The required level on scenes not yet loaded (20 instances, three deep) appears whole at tick 1, its command's tick, the gate having held the world for 7 polls over the run; the optional level, building and room on scenes already loaded (depths three, two and one) each appear whole at tick 3, their command's tick; the required building at tick 8; the two reloads whole at ticks 40 and 56. The optional level on scenes not yet loaded appears over four ticks (8 to 18) and the optional one whose rooms are at another hash over three (12 to 16), parents first. No tick completes with a required instance missing, in the worlds and in the oracles.
- **Hypothesis 5 holds locally.** The 24 rooms of the two levels whose room payload was replaced under its unchanged building fail with the reason (24 of 24), none is spawned, their vaults and buildings are, and the world steps; the import of that level refuses the same state by name.
- **Hypothesis 6 holds locally.** At the two checkpoints, 18 523 full references at the standard size and 74 053 at the dense one (2 574 and 7 832 of them entities an override changed) resolve to the entity that holds them, by each of the three places, with 0 changed. Beside the world without overrides at the end (8 678 entities against 8 002 at the standard size), 7 656 full references are held by both, 1 050 of them overridden; 422 are gone by an override that removes the entity, 600 with the vault whose instancing entity an override removes, 346 exist by an override that adds them, and none is unexplained (30 866, 3 268, 1 182, 2 400, 1 106 and 0 at the dense size).

A spawn costs about 0.1 microsecond per entity by the prepared payload and by the ordered application in the world, and about 0.15 by the staging area; preparing the 57 payloads takes 0.7 and 2.6 milliseconds. Single runs, an order of magnitude only.

The result holds for the reference workload version 1's kernels on this fixture at this toolchain, on the founder's machine; the five cells follow.
