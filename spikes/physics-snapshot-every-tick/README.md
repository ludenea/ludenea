# Physics snapshot at every tick

Throwaway spike of the networking and multiplayer research. An engine that predicts a physics world and corrects it when an authority disagrees must put the world back to an earlier tick and apply the ticks again, inside one frame. This spike measures, instead of assuming, what that costs with the two physics libraries the research studies: the cost of taking the state after every tick, the cost of a whole correction inside one frame, whether the world still continues exactly after a restore repeated at every frame, and what remains where a take at every tick is too dear. The measurement is what a decision on whether rollback of the physics world is required rests on; that decision is not this spike's.

## Hypothesis

A correction restores the engine's components and the library's world, so a take has three parts, timed apart: the library's image of its world, the tables that travel with it (a map from each entity to its library handle, with a digest of the entity's definition), and the canonical bytes of the components the engine would own for the same bodies (definition, transform, motion, sleep flag), written in entity order. The fixed step is one sixtieth of a second, 16.67 ms. Eight claims, per library, each with what refutes it:

- **A take.** For a world of 1 000 bodies, taking the three parts after every tick costs at most a tenth of the fixed step, 1.67 ms. Refuted on a Linux or Windows cell, or on the founder's machine, where the median over the run exceeds it in any of the three scenes. The largest body count for which it holds is reported.
- **A correction inside one frame.** For a world of 1 000 bodies at 4 workers, a restore in place, eight ticks applied again with the three parts taken after each, and the frame's new tick fit in one fixed step. Refuted where the 99th percentile over 1 000 corrections exceeds 16.67 ms in any of the three scenes. Reported as well at depths of 2 and of 24 ticks, with the largest body count that fits at each depth.
- **Continuation.** After each of 1 000 corrections of one to eight ticks, one at every frame, the world's transforms and motion hash, at every tick, as those of a world never restored that received the same inputs, at 1 and 4 workers, on every cell. Refuted by one differing hash.
- **The ring.** The memory of a ring of ten takes is ten times the size of one take measured in the same run, within a tenth; and the image of a world after 10 000 ticks in which bodies are destroyed and created is at most a tenth larger than the image of a fresh world with the same bodies. Refuted beyond either tenth.
- **A sleeping world.** A world most of whose bodies sleep costs as much to take and weighs as much as the same world awake, within a tenth. Refuted if either falls by more than a tenth, in which case a quiet world is cheaper than its body count says and the sizes reported are those of awake worlds.
- **A take every fourth tick.** Evaluated at the smallest body count of the ladder at which the claim on a take fails: the tick that takes still fits (its step and its take together within one fixed step at the 99th percentile), and a correction fits in one frame with the three ticks more it may then have to apply. Refuted where either does not hold; not evaluated where the claim on a take fails at no size.
- **The confirmed tick alone.** For a world of 1 000 bodies at 4 workers, with a ring that holds one take, a restore in place, eight ticks applied again with the three parts taken once on the way, and the frame's new tick fit in one fixed step. Refuted where the 99th percentile over 1 000 corrections exceeds 16.67 ms in any of the three scenes.
- **Write-back**, the correction that needs no image: the components of eight ticks ago are restored, the library's world is left at the present tick, the restored transforms and motion are written into its bodies and the eight ticks are applied again. It is not a continuation (refuted if the hashes equal the uninterrupted world's in every scene). It stays close: after the eight ticks no body is further from its place in the uninterrupted world than a hundredth of its own size, and 600 ticks later the highest body of a pile lies within one body's size of its height in the uninterrupted world (refuted by one body beyond the hundredth or by a pile that fell). Recorded and deciding nothing: the first tick at which the hashes part and whether they meet again, which is what a replay of the confirmed history would see.

## Method

Two programs, one per library, at the versions the research names. The Box2D half is a C17 program (`box2d/`) that fetches Box2D at tag `v3.2.0` through CMake and takes its image with `b2World_GetSnapshot`, restores it with `b2World_RestoreFromSnapshot`, as the Box2D snapshot continuation spike did; `setup.sh` builds it before the Cargo build. The Rapier half is this crate, on `rapier3d` 0.36.1 with the `enhanced-determinism`, `parallel` and `serde-serialize` features, its image written by `bincode` 1.3.3 and restored in place by replacing the world's state and keeping its pipelines, as the Rapier snapshot continuation spike did. The Rust program runs the C program as a child and prints one JSON document with both halves. The two halves run the same experiments on scenes built alike in 2D and in 3D.

- **Scenes that scale**, at 100, 300, 1 000, 3 000 and 10 000 bodies, sleeping off: a pile at rest (pyramids of boxes ten wide on a ground), bodies in motion that seldom touch (balls, boxes and capsules four units apart inside walls, no gravity, no friction, elastic contacts), and chains of jointed bodies (chains of ten links hung from a fixed anchor and released horizontal, links that collide with nothing).
- **Inputs.** At every tick, impulses on four bodies drawn from a seeded stream, a pure function of the tick, so that a tick applied again receives what it received; a mispredicted tick pushes one body the other way three times as hard.
- **A tick** is the inputs, the library's step and the read back of every body's transform, motion and sleep flag into the components. The read back is timed apart and is not counted in a take: an engine reads its components back whether or not it takes anything.
- **A take after every tick.** Each scene and size steps 600 ticks after a warm-up of 60, at 1 and at 4 workers, taking the three parts after each tick into a ring of ten. Reported: the median and the 99th percentile of the step, of the read back, of each part, of the take and of the tick with its take; the bytes of each part per body; the memory the ring holds, which is the capacity of its buffers, each grown to the largest take it received and never beyond.
- **A correction inside one frame**, at 4 workers, per scene and size, in six forms: takes at every tick at depths 2, 8 and 24, takes every second and every fourth tick at depth 8 (a correction then starts from the last take at or before the tick it goes back to, so it applies up to one or three ticks more), and the confirmed tick alone at depth 8 (one take per frame, of the tick that becomes the confirmed one). A frame is a restore in place, the ticks applied again with their takes, and the frame's new tick with its take. A run is 1 000 corrections; it stops once at least 100 are run and 11 exceeded the fixed step, since the 99th percentile over 1 000 is then over it whatever the others measure. Reported: the median, the 99th percentile and the largest frame, the restore, a tick applied again, the first tick applied after the restore timed apart as well, and the count of corrections run. For takes at every tick at depth 8 the final hash is compared with a world never restored that stepped the same ticks.
- **Continuation**, on the scenes of the two snapshot continuation spikes at their sizes: Box2D's own `joint_grid`, `large_pyramid`, `many_pyramids`, `smash` and `tumbler` (2 026 to 22 001 bodies), and the three 3D scenes of the Rapier spike, `large_pyramid`, `joint_grid` and `mixed_shape_rain` (1 441 to 2 500 bodies). At each of 1 000 frames, after a warm-up of 8 ticks, a depth is drawn from one to eight, the world is restored to that many ticks ago, the ticks are applied again with the true inputs and taken, and the frame's new tick is applied, mispredicted one time in two. A correction that follows a mispredicted tick amends it; the others apply the same inputs again. Every tick, once it holds its true inputs, is hashed (transform and motion of every body, in entity order) and compared with the uninterrupted world, which received the true inputs from the start.
- **The churn**: the bodies in motion at 1 000 bodies, in which every tick destroys ten bodies drawn from a seeded stream and creates ten, for 10 000 ticks; the fresh world is created from the components of the churned world's last tick, and the two images are compared.
- **The sleeping world**: the pile at 1 000 bodies with sleeping on and no input, stepped until no body is awake; 200 takes of it are timed; then every body is woken, one step is taken so that the awake world carries its contact constraints, and 200 takes are timed again.
- **Write-back**: each scaling scene at 1 000 bodies, at 1 and at 4 workers; after 128 ticks the components of tick 120 are written into the bodies, the eight ticks are applied again with the same inputs, then 600 more, each tick compared with the uninterrupted world.
- **The world taken as a copy of its state types**, a variant of the Rapier half added on 2026-11-13. The library's world is taken by copying its state types (the sets of bodies, colliders and joints, the island manager, the broad phase, the narrow phase, the parameters: what the image holds, each type copied by the copy it derives) in place of serializing it, and restored by copying that back into the world, whose pipelines are kept as they are with the image. The ring keeps its copy, so a restore makes a second one. A copy holds the workspace fields the serializer skips and a restore from an image builds again, so the state it restores is not the state an image restores. **Hypothesis**: a restore from a copy costs a fraction of a restore from the image, and the world continues identically. Three experiments run with it beside the image's in the same run: the take after every tick at 1 000 bodies, at 1 and 4 workers; the correction of eight ticks inside one frame, with takes at every tick and with the confirmed tick alone, at 1 000, 3 000 and 10 000 bodies and 4 workers; and the continuation on the three library scenes. The memory a copy holds is the library's own, in as many allocations as its types make, and is not measured.

`cargo test` and the C program's `--self-check` verify the drivers' own logic (hash vectors, a world restored and stepped again equals the world never restored, from an image and from a copy, a mispredicted input changes what follows, the report holds every section). `--quick` runs a tenth of everything; `--library box2d` or `--library rapier` runs one half and `--only takes`, `corrections`, `continuation`, `copy`, `churn`, `asleep` or `write_back` one experiment. `ci/summarize.sh physics-snapshot-every-tick <out directory>` derives the verdict of each claim from the figures and prints the tables.

What is read where the specification was silent, and what differs from it: a run of corrections stops once its verdict is settled, as said above; cadences of two and four are measured at every size, so that the claim on a take every fourth tick is read at whichever size the claim on a take fails on each machine; the ring's memory is the memory its buffers hold, not the process's; the tables and the canonical bytes are the spike's own formats.

Relationship to the external components: Box2D v3.2.0 (MIT) and Rapier 3D 0.36.1 (Apache 2.0) are the subjects of the measurement, studied here and integrated nowhere; `bincode` 1.3.3 (MIT) is the serializer the Rapier image needs and `rayon` 1 the pool that library accepts; nothing of them is vendored or reused beyond this spike.

Its limits, stated before it ran: the scenes are synthetic; the tables and the canonical bytes are the spike's own, not an engine's adapter or storage; no engine scheduler steps the world; the hosted runners' timings are those of shared machines; a result holds for these two library versions and this toolchain.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=physics-snapshot-every-tick`, and the founder's machine (AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 logical processors, Windows 11). Release builds at the pinned Rust toolchain. The Box2D half is built with the compiler each image gives by default (GCC on Linux, Apple Clang on macOS, MSVC on Windows for x86_64), `-ffp-contract=off` on GCC and Clang, and with Clang on Windows for ARM64 (`clang-cl` with the two flags Box2D v3.2.0 needs there, see the Box2D worker sweep spike's README), since foreign code inside the deterministic domain is built with GCC or Clang on every cell and with MSVC on x86_64 only. Timings compare per processor only. The macOS cell counts for the continuation claim and is reported without a verdict for the timed claims.

The cause of the dearer take of a sleeping Box2D world was not looked for.
