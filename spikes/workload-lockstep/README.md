# Reference workload lockstep

Throwaway spike of the networking and multiplayer research. It runs two instances of the [reference workload](../reference-workload/README.md) as peers in deterministic lockstep over a simulated link with latency, jitter and loss, each peer owning one input stream of kicks, and measures, instead of assuming, whether the two worlds' per-tick hashes stay identical at every worker count for input delays of 2 and 4 ticks and a loss rate up to 10 percent, whether they are the sequential oracle's hashes for the merged records, whether a divergence injected in one peer is detected by the hash exchange at its tick and named by its first divergent system from the two retained states, how many frames a peer stalls per link setting, and what a rollback variant costs per rollback.

## Layout

- `src/world.rs`: the [history capture](../workload-history-capture/README.md) spike's world trimmed to what two peers need: the reference workload on the dependency-counting tick graph, the input fixed point of the [control surface toy](../control-surface-toy/README.md) spike (a tick's ordered record of kicks, validated against the state at the boundary and applied before the first system), the snapshot of the [runtime skeleton](../runtime-skeleton/README.md) spike (the canonical bytes of a tick boundary, restored into a new world), the sequential oracle, and the hash of each component group of a state (velocities, positions, the structural columns and the stream, the energy) in the order the systems write them.
- `src/lockstep.rs`: the peers, the link and the protocol. Time is virtual: a frame is the slot of one tick, nothing sleeps and no socket opens. A peer issues its input for tick `t + D` after stepping tick `t` (four kicks on entities drawn from its seeded stream, with the generation it sees locally and deltas in [-8, 8]; the inputs for ticks 1 to `D` are issued at startup) and steps tick `t` only when it holds both peers' inputs for `t`, merged in the canonical order by source identity (peer 0's kicks, then peer 1's, each in issue order); otherwise it stalls the frame. The link delivers a message `latency + jitter` frames after it was sent, the jitter drawn uniformly in `0..=jitter_max` and the loss as a drop probability, from a seeded stream. Every frame each peer sends one message carrying every input of its own the other peer has not acknowledged (the redundancy is the reliable layer), its acknowledgement and the hash of its latest confirmed tick; a hash that differs from the local one is a detected divergence. The injected desync is a kick peer 1 applies at a chosen tick and never sends. The rollback variant on peer 1 predicts the remote input it lacks (the last one received), steps ahead within a window of eight ticks past its confirmed tick, keeps a ring of per-tick snapshots, and when a real input contradicts a prediction restores the snapshot of the tick before it into a new world and re-simulates to its current tick.
- `src/main.rs`: the scenarios and the report.

## Hypotheses

With the reference workload, version 1, default size (10 000 entities, 1 000 ticks), wander off, both peers from the same seed, four kicks per tick per peer, compiled by one pinned Rust toolchain at default target features:

1. Two peers in lockstep over a link with latency, jitter and loss give identical per-tick hashes at every tick, at 1 and 4 workers per world (and the machine's parallelism) on every cell, for input delays of 2 and 4 ticks and the link settings (latency frames, jitter maximum, loss) of (0, 0, 0), (1, 0, 0), (2, 1, 2 percent), (4, 1, 10 percent) and (6, 2, 10 percent); and the hashes are the sequential oracle's for the merged records, which are the same values whatever the link and the worker count.
2. A kick applied by peer 1 at tick 500 without being sent is detected by the hash exchange at tick 500 (reported with the frame of detection and the peers that detected it), the first divergent tick is 500, the first divergent system named from the two states of that tick is the input fixed point (the velocities group, then the positions and the energy, the structural columns identical), and the two snapshots of tick 500 are kept and differ.
3. Without the injection the same run reports no divergence, and no lockstep run reports one.
4. The input delay bounds the latency: with a latency at or below the delay and no loss, the peers never stall after the first inputs' travel; with a latency above it, every tick waits for the link, measured as the stall frames per setting (the speed control of Age of Empires as a measurement); losses add the retransmission's frames.
5. The rollback peer keeps its confirmed ticks' hashes equal to the lockstep peer's with a latency above the delay, every rollback costing a restore plus the re-simulated ticks, measured per rollback with the ticks re-simulated per rollback bounded by the latency plus the jitter; with a latency below the delay it never rolls back.

## Method

Per worker count, per delay, per link setting: one session of 1 000 ticks without snapshots, reporting whether both peers' hashes are identical (and the first divergent tick), whether both run hashes equal the oracle's (computed once per delay from the first run's records), the stall frames of each peer, the frames used (frame 0 is the exchange of the pre-issued inputs), the messages sent, delivered and dropped, the bytes sent and per tick per peer, the kicks applied and stale, the hashes compared, and the elapsed time with both worlds' ticks per second. Once, at 4 workers (the machine's parallelism when smaller), delay 2, latency 1: the injection run with a ring of 16 snapshots per peer (the run stops at the detection), then the same run without injection. Then the rollback variant, delay 2, ring of 8, over the links (1, 0, 0), (3, 1, 0) and (5, 1, 5 percent): the rollbacks, the predicted and mispredicted ticks, the re-simulated ticks (mean and maximum), the rollback time (mean, maximum and total), the stall frames of both peers, whether the confirmed hashes match the lockstep peer's and the oracle's, and the per-tick snapshot cost of the ring (the history capture spike's measurement at cadence 1, repeated here).

The self-checks (`cargo test -p workload-lockstep`) verify on the small size that the world without kicks reproduces the sequential reference and with kicks agrees with the oracle at 1, 2 and 3 workers in batches of 1, 7 and 40, that a snapshot restores into a world that continues identically, that two peers over a perfect link give identical hashes at every tick equal to the oracle's for the merged records, that a latency above the delay stalls and keeps the hashes identical and a latency at the delay stalls for the first inputs' travel only, that a link losing a fifth of its messages delivers every input through the redundancy, that the local-only kick is detected at its tick with the velocities group first and the retained snapshots differing, that the rollback peer rolls back under a latency above the delay with its confirmed hashes equal to the lockstep peer's and never under a latency below it, that the link's draws are deterministic for a seed, that the message encoding round trips and refuses truncated or trailing bytes, that the group hashes change only the group a change touches, and that the whole report runs with every check true.

## Environment

Research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=workload-lockstep`. Rust toolchain pinned in [`rust-toolchain.toml`](../rust-toolchain.toml), release profile, default target features of each target; plus a local run on an Apple M5 (10 cores, macOS 26) outside the matrix. The two worlds step one after the other in the frame loop, so they never share the cores at one time; each tick is one step call, whose worker threads are spawned per step as in the skeleton spike. Timings from hosted runners compare per processor only.
