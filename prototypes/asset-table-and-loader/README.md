# Asset table and loader

Throwaway prototype of the assets research. Never product code, and never a first version of the engine.

It builds, from the text of a design alone, a world's **asset table** and the **loading capability** that answers it, and checks that what was built gives the states the text says. An entity names an asset by a 128-bit identifier and asks for nothing; what a world loads, it loads because a **want** says so: a record of the table that names an asset, an owner and a policy (required or optional). An asset becomes available, or failed, by an **arrival** applied at a tick's input fixed point; it falls when no want names it; and a world does not step past a tick boundary while a required asset is unanswered (the **gate**). The loading capability resolves each want in the manifest the world stands under, the instance's current one or the manifest of a **content set** the world names, and posts the arrivals of a want's closure as one batch.

Two spikes measured the parts one by one. This prototype exists because what a reading of such a design gets wrong is how its mechanisms meet: the table with the gate, the loader with a content set, a restore with a revision.

## What it stands on

- The [asset table spike](../../spikes/workload-asset-table/README.md): its table, its world on the reference workload, its saved world read by another process. **Copied**, then changed where the design text states a rule the spike did not hold; the spike stays as measured. The prototype's first check is that spike's scenarios run again, with their oracles, on the changed table and world (`spike-check/`).
- The [import graph spike](../../spikes/asset-import-graph/README.md): its artifact cache, its action keyed by what it reads, its domain manifest, its revision put in place by one rename, its package and its mount with and without leave to override. **Copied**, without its sources and importers: the world this prototype steps reads the canonical part of the asset table spike, so the stand-in pipeline has one importer of its own, for a small text source. Nothing of the pipeline is measured here.
- The [reference workload](../../spikes/reference-workload/README.md), version 1, the repulsion parameters read through an asset as the asset table spike read them.

Standard library and the reference workload crate alone.

**Stand-ins for what does not exist.** The engine's world, its systems and its canonical form: the reference workload's world. The composition and its contributors: a list of the harness. Asset types: one synthetic type of parameters, a second one so that a composition can lack a type, and a third of which no asset exists, which a composition or a request alone names. A content hash: the 64-bit function of the spikes. The I/O executor: two plain file threads behind the store port. The step of a control surface: a function call. A scene instance: a record of the harness that counts instantiations and makes its want under the world's own entry.

The same-harness stage adds stand-ins and no new kind of machinery:

- **A system that makes wants**: one scripted system of the stand-in world, brought by a contributor of its own. Per tick it makes a want, releases one or asks for the despawn of an entity. The stand-in holds what the system does as a list per tick, which the world applies where the structural changes of a system are applied: it is no node of the tick's graph, and it declares no access. It is part of the world and no input: a replay runs it again.
- **A world's budget for its asset data**: a count of bytes in the world's configuration. The size of a content is the length of its canonical part, which the domain manifest holds and to which the stand-in pipeline pads the part. The unit the text proposes has an arrival name its size and the entry hold it: neither does here, and the core reads the length of the part under that unit as under the three candidates.
- **A reason of failure for an arrival rejected over a budget**, and **the mark an arrival carries under one candidate of the budget** (the asset of the want it answers). The design text names neither: both exist for the comparison of the candidates alone.
- **A part the stand-in pipeline refuses to write** (it lists a dependency under another type than the manifest holds it under), which the harness puts in the store itself.
- **A delay before a batch is posted**: none, a delay that makes the batches of one round ready in the reverse of the order in which their wants left, or a delay drawn from the clock.

## Layout

One Cargo workspace whose crates follow the placement the design gives the subsystem. The harness records the dependency graph and checks it: the table has no edge to the loading capability, and the loading capability none to the pipeline.

- `core/`: the asset identity core inside the stand-in world. `table.rs` holds the table, each entry with its asset type and its wants in their total order, the input record, the gate, the candidates of a budget and the canonical bytes; `world.rs` the world, its input fixed point, its scripted system, its save and its two restores. It reads no file and reaches a part through an extension point. Every rule a scenario's control changes on purpose, and every place where the text was read one way among several, is a field of `Rules`.
- `store/`: the content store library. The formats the loading capability and the pipeline share and nothing else: the domain manifest, the root document of a revision, the package. No thread, no I/O.
- `loader/`: the loading capability. The resident store, the store port and its file threads, the manifest resolved per world, one batch per world and per want that leaves, the moves of the manifest (a revision, a mount, an unmount), the answers to a restore and the confirmation of a restore under the recorded content. Its controls and its readings are the fields of `Options`.
- `pipeline/`: the stand-in pipeline.
- `spike-check/`: the asset table spike's scenarios, copied and ported.
- `harness/`: the scenarios, the oracles and the report. Two sheets state the inputs of every scenario and the format of an oracle, and no expected result: [`harness/scenarios.md`](harness/scenarios.md) for the families of the first stage and of the rules amended after it, [`harness/scenarios-same-harness.md`](harness/scenarios-same-harness.md) for the five claims of the same-harness stage. `harness/oracles/` holds one oracle per family. In `harness/src/`, `families.rs` and `families_same_harness.rs` hold each sheet as data, in the sheet's own words; `play.rs` plays a scenario and judges what it gave apart from playing it; `oracle.rs` reads an oracle and compares; `gate_cost.rs` measures the one claim that is a magnitude; `enumeration.rs` enumerates small records.

## Hypothesis

Stated before the run. The asset table and the loading capability can be built from the design text alone, and so built they give the states the text says: every scenario equals an oracle written by hand from the same text, at 1, 4 and 8 workers, in release and unoptimized builds.

Two kinds of result refute it, and each is a finding about the text and not a failure of the build:

- **A differing result**: a state hash, the canonical bytes of a table, a list of events or a step's result that differs from the oracle's, once the difference is traced to the text and not to a fault of the build.
- **A rule that cannot be built as stated**: two sentences that give two behaviors, a case no sentence covers, a rule that needs a fact the text places elsewhere.

A build that equals its oracles shows that the scenarios run hold, on stand-ins. It does not show that no other mechanism is wrong.

## The four claims of the first stage

| Claim | Scenarios | Refuted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The order at a tick's input fixed point gives the stated states: the record first, its commands before its arrivals, then the falls | A want and the arrival of its asset in one record; a release and an arrival at a new hash in one record; a want made again at the tick of a fall, after a release and after a despawn; each of those records as the pending record of a gated world; three pending records on a world gated on one required asset | One state hash that differs from the oracle's |
| A want's owner orders the table | A parent wanted by two entities, one required and one optional, with one dependency, the two wants made in one order and in the other; a parent whose hash changes to a part with another dependency; a host's preload over an asset the composition requires, then released; one maker that wants one asset twice | One state hash that differs, or an asset that falls while a maker still wants it |
| A named content set holds under a moved manifest | A world created under a set beside a world that names none, and the same world under the same set on a second instance whose manifest never moves; a revision, the removal of an artifact, a mount with leave to override and an unmount moved under them; four required wants made meanwhile; a recording made with no set replayed under a set | A want answered at a hash outside the set, or a gate that does not open |
| A history is never continued under other content, on a restore under the content at hand | One world saved with available assets, a failed entry, an unanswered required want and an entry with no want; nine readers, each a process of its own over a store of its own: the same content under another set, one asset changed, one asset absent, the entries that are not available under a want, the unanswered want answered at another hash, a revision that lands while the world waits, a parent whose part names another dependency, another composition with and without the drop named | A first tick that runs before every answer, an event or a new instantiation on a marked answer, a marked answer replaced by a revision, a continued run whose hashes differ from the uninterrupted run's, or one state hash that differs from the oracle's |

## The same-harness stage

The design text was amended on what the first stage found, and this stage builds the amended rules before it measures anything new. The four families of the first stage run again, their oracles written again under the text as amended. Six families hold the scenarios of the amended rules that no run had taken:

| Family | What its scenarios turn on |
| --- | --- |
| A want added on a failed entry | The entry returns to unavailable when the want is applied, so that a retry is state: it gates where required, and a save holds it. Eight scenarios, two of them readers of a save |
| The asset type an entry holds | The first want writes the type; a want that states another is a recorded refusal; an entry failed for its type takes the type of the next want that states another; the drop rule of a restore reads the type of each entry. Eleven scenarios, three of them readers |
| The wants of dependencies where no batch brings them | A parent wanted again at the tick of its fall, a required want added on a parent in force, a closure over a dependency the table holds available |
| What the batch of a revision holds | The closure of a changed part, the former dependencies of a parent, an entry that holds no want |
| The gate over a parent pending without its dependency | A scripted source posts what no loading capability posts: a parent without the assets of its closure |
| Elements of a record that change nothing, and the kind of a maker | An arrival that leaves an entry as it was, a want that stands after the despawn of its owner, two makers of one identity |

It then takes five more claims, on the same harness and the same stand-ins:

| Claim | Scenarios | Refuted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A want made by a system through its declared access behaves as one made by a command | The asset table spike's scenarios that turn on wants and on the gate during a run, the wants made by the scripted system; one world by the system beside one by commands; a want in the tick of an arrival of its asset, and in the tick of its asset's fall | One state hash that differs from the oracle's |
| Several worlds on one loader each receive their own batches | Sixteen worlds on one instance, four patterns of records each held by four worlds one tick apart, two patterns under a named content set, closures that overlap; run with no delay, with the batches of a round ready in reverse order, and with delays drawn from the clock | A world that receives an arrival another world's want asked for, or a state that depends on a delay |
| A mount while worlds exist reaches each world as one revision | Five worlds under a package mounted with leave to override and then unmounted: one that holds the overridden asset, one that wants it later, one that wanted the package's own asset before the mount, one that released both, one under a named content set; a package whose own asset lists the overridden one | A world that receives the mount as several batches, or a world under a set that receives it at all |
| An excess over a world's budget is rejected identically | Six cases, each under three candidates of what is rejected: the single arrival, the closure of one want, the whole drain of a tick. The design text leaves the choice open and the stage chooses none. Each candidate showed a defect, and the unit the text then proposed, the closure of a want with its bounds derived by the core and no mark in the record, runs as a fourth candidate on the six cases and on two of its own: a replacement whose part lists a new dependency, and two closures that each fit alone | One state hash that differs from the oracle's, under any candidate |
| A gate evaluated on a large table costs under a tenth of a tick | A table of ten thousand assets, two sizes of pending record, the gate open, closed and opening; a hundred evaluations set against a hundred ticks of the same world, at 1, 4 and 8 workers | A tenth of a tick or more. It is a magnitude: it has no oracle, and it is judged on one quiet machine, in the release profile, over five repetitions |

## Method

- **Two readings of one text.** The oracles were written by hand from the design text, by writers who had read neither this build nor the code of the spikes it stands on, and were in place before the scenario each judges first ran. The build was written from the same text by someone who had not read the oracles. Where the two differ, the text arbitrates, with three outcomes: the build misread, the oracle misread, or the text allows both.
- **An oracle is a table of states and not a second implementation**: per named tick, the elements of the tick's input record, the whole asset table with every want in its stated order, the events, the instances, and what a step names. The harness writes each stated table through the canonical form, in the order the oracle gives, and compares bytes; the state hash it expects for a tick is the hash of those bytes with the workload's hash of a sequential run that reads, per asset, what the stated table gives.
- **Each scenario has a control**: the same scenario with the mechanism under test deliberately changed (the arrivals before the commands, the falls before the record, one shared want under the world's entry, the instance's manifest in place of the set's, the answers to a restore posted unmarked, and so on) must differ from the oracle. A control that equals the oracle is a fault of the scenario.
- **A reading is run both ways.** Where the text gives two behaviors, or none, the build takes one reading and the harness runs the other as well, so that a difference between the build and an oracle that comes from a reading alone is shown to be that and nothing else.
- **A reading taken becomes a control.** Where the founder has taken one reading of such a point, the oracles stand under it, and the reading not taken is run as a control the sheet names, which must differ from the oracle. Three points stand so since 2026-11-14, each the build's default: the wants the core added for the dependencies of a parent that fails, or whose arrival is rejected, end; the wants of a parent's former dependencies end once the arrivals of the batch are applied; a step on a gated world names the dependencies that keep the gate closed, the pending record counted. The last changed the build, which first named what the state holds unanswered, the reading now run as the control of `pending-parent/required`.
- **A replay of each run.** The input log of every scenario, read back from its bytes, replays the run at another worker count to the same state hashes.
- **An enumeration of small records.** Every record of at most four elements over two assets and two owners, applied to the table alone from five starting tables and followed by one empty tick, is checked against invariants the text states: no asset falls while a want names it; no entry stays without a want past one tick boundary; the canonical bytes do not depend on the order in which wants were made; a table read back from its bytes is byte-identical. A second enumeration adds the elements of the amended rules (a want that states the second type, a failure for the type, a starting table failed for its type) and four invariants those rules state; since the first want of an asset writes its type, it counts apart the records in which the order of two wants of two types decides a type.
- A live loading capability answers on a thread of its own; the host waits, before each step, until it has nothing left to post, so that the tick an answer lands in is a function of the scenario.

What the method gained with the same-harness stage:

- **The sheets are data.** A scenario is its sheet's statement, element by element in the sheet's words, and one player runs every family. Playing a scenario and judging what it gave are apart, so that every scenario and every control can be driven with no oracle: the self-checks do, and compare nothing with an oracle.
- **Several worlds stepped in rounds.** In a round the host steps every world of a scenario one tick, in the order the scenario lists them, without waiting between two worlds; before each round it waits until the loading capability has nothing left to post. What moves the content (a revision, a mount, an unmount, the repair or the removal of an artifact) happens between two rounds. A step taken before the capability has posted is made by holding the capability still while the tick before runs.
- **A scripted system.** What it does in a tick is part of the scenario, and the fixed point at which it is applied is a reading the builder and the writer of the oracle each took alone; two controls apply it elsewhere.
- **The candidates of a budget** are one rule with a value for each, the unit the text proposes being the fourth, so that each case runs once under each, and a control under which the budget only reports.
- **A save read in another process, under the build as changed.** A control or a reading changes the build of every process: the run that saved runs under it too.
- **Every reading on every scenario.** The readings the build did not take, on points the founder has not decided since, are run, each alone, on every scenario that differs from its oracle; a mode of the harness runs each on every scenario and lists the scenarios that tell it apart from the build as it stands.
- **A magnitude beside the states.** The cost of the gate is measured with the time of its copy of the table apart, and with the one case in which the build reads no pending record measured again under the reading in which it does.

A run prints one JSON document of facts on standard output and a short summary on standard error, and writes its full log, every difference with an oracle in words, beside its facts. The self-checks (`cargo test --workspace`) drive every scenario and every control of every family on the small size, the readers of a save inside the test process, to its last tick and through a replay, and hold the self-checks of each crate; none compares anything with an oracle. Four modes of the harness compare nothing with an oracle either: the first check alone, the enumeration alone, the cost of the gate alone, and every scenario driven with what each world gave written in words.

## Environment

The research CI, workflow `research-ci.yml`, five cells (see [`spikes/README.md`](../../spikes/README.md)), run on demand with `-f spike=asset-table-and-loader`, in the release profile (reference workload at default size, 10 000 entities) and in the unoptimized profile (`-f profile=dev`, the small size, 1 000 entities), which judges identity and no magnitude. One quiet machine outside the matrix (an AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 16 cores and 32 threads, Windows 11). Rust toolchain pinned in [`spikes/rust-toolchain.toml`](../../spikes/rust-toolchain.toml), default target features. Worker counts 1, 4 and 8.

## Result

**The first stage, on the five cells and on one machine.** Measured on 2026-11-14 on the five cells, in the release profile (run 43579133175, default size) and in the unoptimized profile (run 43579135961, small size), and the same day on the quiet machine, three times in the release profile (default size, a run of 36 seconds) and once in the unoptimized profile (small size). Every run gives the same verdicts, scenario by scenario, on every cell and on the machine. Each of the thirty scenario worlds, the scenarios with a live loading capability included, has one run hash across the five cells, the machine and its three release runs at the default size, and one across the five cells and the machine at the small size. The lines below are true of every cell.

- **The first check holds**: the six scenarios of the asset table spike that are run again (wants, the gate during a run, a revision as one batch, the table under the hash, a save read by another process, one resident copy) hold on the changed table and world, each reader of the save a process of its own. Two things were adapted and no check was weakened: the count of reload events on the answers to a restore counts arrivals and changes of hash, the event that records a new history being a kind the spike did not have; and the readers of the save run under the reading the spike measured for a failed answer to a restore (an event, and a failed instance), which the prototype keeps as one reading among three.
- **The order at a tick's input fixed point**: the eleven scenarios equal their oracle at 1, 4 and 8 workers, tick by tick (the record, the table's bytes, the events, what a step names, the state hash), each log replays at another worker count, and the ten controls differ.
- **The owners of wants**: four scenarios of five equal their oracle and the six controls differ. The fifth, the preload over the composition, differs at three ticks in the order of two wants of one entry, one made by a contributor and one by the source of a command: the build ordered the kinds, the oracle the numeric identities. Run under the oracle's reading, the build equals it at every tick.
- **A named content set under a moved manifest**: the world under the set, the world that names none and the world on the second instance each equal their oracle, the world under the set has the state hashes of the world whose manifest never moved (run hash `0xcd6756fc78f64816` for both), a recording made with no set replays under the set to the same hashes with the set's hash in the header of a save, and the two controls differ.
- **A restore under the content at hand**: the saved world and five readers equal their oracle; four readers differ from it by a reading alone, and equal it once run under the oracle's reading (where the event that records a new history stands among the events of its tick, in four readers; whether a failed answer to a restore fails the instance made from the asset, in one). The nine controls differ. Two results are not of that kind:
  - **A reader that never ran.** Where the part at hand of a parent lists another dependency, answers posted for the assets the saved table holds and for nothing else leave the world gated for ever: the gate, evaluated with the pending answers applied, counts the want of the new dependency, nothing is applied while the gate is closed, and so that want never leaves the world to be answered. The build therefore posts the new dependency with the answers, as an ordinary arrival, which is also what the oracle's writer read.
  - **A continued run that differs.** Where the saved table holds a required want unanswered and the content at hand answers it at another hash than the run that went on received, no answer to the restore differs, the restore is not recorded as the start of a new history, and the state hashes differ from the uninterrupted run's from the first tick.
- **The enumeration of small records**: 13 120 records, 65 600 applications, no invariant broken. 1 293 applications reach a case the rule on the fall reads two ways: an entry with no want, wanted and released again inside one record.

**Where the text was read.** The builder and the two writers of oracles recorded, each alone, the places where the text gave two behaviors or none. Those that a run could tell apart were run both ways: which wants a loading capability answers (each new want, or each asset that is unanswered: the input record differs, the state does not); where a despawn of a record ends its entity's wants (at its place in the record, or at the structural change point of its tick: the state differs when the record also holds the arrival of the asset); how two makers of different kinds compare; what a failed answer to a restore does; where the event of a restore stands; whether the answers to a restore follow the closure of the parts at hand.

**The same-harness stage, on the five cells and on one machine.** Measured on 2026-11-14 on the five cells, in the release profile (run 43606611828, default size) and in the unoptimized profile (run 43606615019, small size), and the same day on the quiet machine, once in each profile. Every run gives the same verdicts, scenario by scenario, on every cell and on the machine, and each of the 161 scenario worlds has one run hash across the five cells and the machine at each size. The lines below are true of every cell but where a magnitude is named.

- **The states**: 108 scenarios in fourteen families, 161 worlds judged against an oracle written by hand before the scenario ran. 151 equal their oracle at 1, 4 and 8 workers; 10 differ by a reading of the design text alone, and equal their oracle once run under the other reading; none differs otherwise. The 182 controls all differ, every input log replays at another worker count, and the first check holds.
- **The rules amended on the first stage**: the ten families that build them equal their oracles in 71 worlds of 73. The first stage's hashes and records are no reference for them: an entry now holds its asset type, and a want leaves the world only where its asset is unanswered or failed.
- **Three readings part the build and the oracles, and nothing else does.** Whether the wants the core added for the dependencies of a parent stand once that parent fails or its arrival is rejected (eight worlds); whether, inside one batch, the wants of a parent's former dependencies end at the parent's own arrival, so that the order of two identifiers decides which of them takes its new hash, or once the arrivals of the batch are applied (one world); and what a step on a gated world names, what its state holds unanswered or what keeps the gate closed once the pending record is counted (one world).
- **A want made by a system**: eight worlds of eight equal their oracle. It gates from the same boundary as a want made by a command and is answered at the same tick. It is applied at the structural change fixed point of its own tick, so two orders are not a command's: an arrival of that tick does not answer it, and an asset wanted again at the tick of its fall is gone.
- **Several worlds on one loader**: 48 worlds of 48, the same sixteen statements under no delay, under batches made ready in reverse order and under delays drawn from the clock. No world receives an arrival that another world's want asked for. The host waits for the loading capability before each round.
- **A mount while worlds exist**: each world receives the mount and the unmount as one batch, and the world under a named content set receives neither. Five worlds of six equal their oracle, the sixth by the first reading above.
- **The gate's cost on a table of ten thousand assets: the claim does not hold.** On the quiet machine, over five repetitions, one evaluation costs 8 to 38 ticks of the reference workload (largest ratio 38.3, against a bound of 0.1). The build copies the table and applies the pending record to the copy: the copy alone is 0.31 millisecond, 0.2 to 1.0 of a tick, and the other 11 milliseconds are this build's recomputation over the whole table at each record, no floor. A world that is not gated reads no pending record and answers from its state. On the cells the largest ratio is 24 to 65: every cell stands over the bound.
- **An excess over a world's budget**: no rejection falls at different ticks or changes an asset in force, under any candidate. Each of the three candidates the design text named shows a defect on the six cases: the single arrival leaves a root in force without a dependency; the whole drain fails a small asset, or withholds a small replacement, for the size of another; the closure of one want, as a mark that names one root per arrival, leaves a root without the dependency it shares with a rejected closure. A fourth candidate, the closure of a want with its bounds derived by the core and no mark in the record, shows none of them: its eight worlds equal an oracle written by hand at the first compared run. It leaves one thing open, which a case shows: in identifier order alone, an optional closure takes the room of a required one.
- **The enumeration of small records**: 29 945 records and 179 670 applications with the elements of the amended rules, no invariant broken. With the type in the entry, the table's bytes depend on the order of two wants of different types, the first deciding the type and the second being refused (14 324 applications): the invariant on the order of making holds among wants that are all applied.

**The readings taken, on the five cells and on one machine.** The founder took a reading on each of the three points on 2026-11-14: the wants the core added for the dependencies of a parent end where that parent fails or its arrival is rejected; over the arrivals of one batch, the wants of a parent's former dependencies end once the arrivals are applied; and a step on a gated world names what keeps the gate closed once the pending record is counted. The nine oracles written under the readings not taken were written again by hand before the build ran against them, the build took the third reading as its default, and each reading not taken became a control. Measured the same day on the five cells, in the release profile (run 43626416362, default size) and in the unoptimized profile (run 43626419720, small size), and on the quiet machine, once in each profile.

- **The states**: the 161 worlds equal their oracle at 1, 4 and 8 workers, on every cell and on the machine; none differs, by a reading or otherwise. The 192 controls all differ, the ten readings not taken among them. Each world has one run hash across the five cells and the machine at each size, the first check holds, and the enumeration of small records breaks no invariant.
- **One point the rule leaves to a reading, taken alike by the oracles and by the build**: whether a step names, as a failed required asset, a dependency that failed under a required want which ended later at the same fixed point. Both name it. Five worlds of the budget turn on it for the name alone; their tables and events are the same either way.
- **The gate's cost is unchanged in kind**: on the quiet machine the largest ratio of one evaluation to one tick is 40.5, against a bound of a tenth; on the cells it is 20.8 to 57.8. The claim stays refuted on a build that copies the table.
- **Not built**: every other reading the founder took the same day, the form of the gate that copies nothing among them. They wait for the last stage of this prototype.

The result holds for the reference workload version 1 at this toolchain, on the five cells and this machine, on the stand-ins named above.
