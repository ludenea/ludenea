# Family first-step-arrival: an arrival for an entry with no want that a
# want of the first step names.
# Written by hand from the design text as it reads after its second reading,
# before any run of the stand-in stage, by a writer who has not read the
# build.
# The table's order is the order of the asset identifiers: B, M, P, Z.
# The table is empty before the first block of each scenario that is not a
# reader.
# Record lines stand in the order applied: the commands first, in the order
# the scenario gives them, then the arrivals by asset identifier.
# An entry line with no want line under it is an entry that holds no want.
# The family gives no size: an entry and an arrival state none.
# A live loading capability in every world: the host waits, before each
# step, until the capability has nothing left to post, so what it posts in
# answer to a tick, or to a revision put in place before a tick, stands in
# the record of that next tick.
# The rule under test. Over the arrivals of one record the core first adds
# the wants that the parts of those arrivals list, starting from the part of
# an asset that holds a want once the commands of the record are applied and
# following it downwards to any depth. It then applies the arrivals in
# identifier order, each judged against the entry as it stands when it is
# applied. An arrival for an entry that holds no want changes nothing,
# unless a want applied before it at that fixed point names the asset again:
# a command's of the same record, or one the core adds at the first step.
# The batch of a revision holds the arrival of an entry with no want for
# that, and so do the answers to a restore. An entry that no want names
# falls once the record is applied.


scenario first-step-arrival/revision-with-the-parent

tick 2
record want P parameters source:host required
record want B parameters source:tool optional
record want Z parameters source:tool optional
entry B parameters unavailable
want source:tool optional
entry P parameters unavailable
want source:host required
entry Z parameters unavailable
want source:tool optional

# Three wants left the domain, each with a closure of one asset: the three
# arrivals stand in one record, by identifier.
tick 3
record arrival B B.1
record arrival P P.1
record arrival Z Z.1
entry B parameters available B.1
want source:tool optional
entry P parameters available P.1
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.1
want source:tool optional
event arrived B B.1
event arrived P P.1
event arrived Z Z.1

# The last want of B and the last want of Z end: each keeps its entry, in
# force and with no want, through this tick.
tick 5
record release B source:tool
record release Z source:tool
entry B parameters available B.1
entry P parameters available P.1
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.1

# The revision changes B, P and Z, and the table of the end of tick 5 names
# the three, two of them with no want: the batch holds the three arrivals.
# The new part of P lists B and Z, which the table holds available, so the
# batch holds no arrival more.
# The record is applied before the falls. P holds a want and the part of its
# arrival lists B and Z: the core first adds the want of each, owned by P
# and required as the want of P is. Then the arrivals, by identifier. B
# stands before P: its arrival is applied before the arrival of P, on an
# entry that a want added at the first step already names, so B takes its
# new hash, with the event of a change. P takes its new hash. Z takes its
# new hash by the same rule. The recomputation leaves both wants: the part
# in force lists B and Z. Each holds a want when the falls are made, and
# neither falls.
tick 6
record arrival B B.2
record arrival P P.2
record arrival Z Z.2
entry B parameters available B.2
want asset:P required
entry P parameters available P.2
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.2
want asset:P required
event changed B B.1 B.2
event changed P P.1 P.2
event changed Z Z.1 Z.2

# The wants the core added were added on assets the table held available:
# nothing leaves the domain. The table of tick 6 stands to tick 10, and the
# records of ticks 7 to 10 are empty.

# The revision changes P and Z, which the table names: the batch holds their
# arrivals. The new part of P lists M, which the table does not name: the
# batch holds the arrival of M, an asset of the closure of a changed part
# that the table does not hold available. The part of M lists Z, which the
# table holds available. The revision does not change B: no arrival.
# The first step follows the parts downwards. P holds a want and its part
# lists M: the core adds the want of M, owned by P and required, the first
# of M, whose entry takes the type the part of P lists. M then holds a want
# and the part of its arrival lists Z: the core adds the want of Z, owned by
# M and required as the want of M is. Then the arrivals, by identifier: M
# rises, with its event; P takes its new hash; the arrival of Z is for an
# entry that a want added at the first step names, two levels under P, and
# it is applied, with the event of a change.
# No want names B: it falls and leaves. Z holds a want and does not fall.
tick 6
record arrival M M.1
record arrival P P.3
record arrival Z Z.2
entry M parameters available M.1
want asset:P required
entry P parameters available P.3
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.2
want asset:M required
event arrived M M.1
event changed P P.1 P.3
event changed Z Z.1 Z.2

# The want of M was answered by an arrival of the batch that brought it, and
# the want of Z was added on an asset the table held available: nothing
# leaves the domain. The table of tick 6 stands to tick 10, and the records
# of ticks 7 to 10 are empty.

# The revision is put in place before tick 5: its batch, built against the
# table of the end of tick 4, in which B and Z each hold the tool's want,
# stands in the record that holds the two releases.
# The commands are applied first: the last want of B and the last want of Z
# end, and each entry holds no want. Then the first step: P holds a want and
# the part of its arrival lists B and Z, so the core adds the want of each,
# owned by P and required.
# Reading taken: an arrival of an asset whose last want a command of the
# same record released is for an entry that holds no want, and it is applied
# where a want added at the first step names the asset again, as the rule on
# such an arrival says of any entry with no want. B, P and Z each take the
# new hash, with the event of a change.
tick 5
record release B source:tool
record release Z source:tool
record arrival B B.2
record arrival P P.2
record arrival Z Z.2
entry B parameters available B.2
want asset:P required
entry P parameters available P.2
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.2
want asset:P required
event changed B B.1 B.2
event changed P P.1 P.2
event changed Z Z.1 Z.2

# Nothing falls at tick 6: B and Z each hold the want their parent owns. The
# table of tick 5 stands to tick 10, and the records of ticks 6 to 10 are
# empty.

# The readers of the save, each under the content at hand, into the saved
# world's composition, naming no set. Before its first tick a reader waits
# for a marked answer to every asset its table holds available, under a want
# or under none: B, P and Z. The save holds no unanswered want.
# Reading taken, as in the families "restore" and "restore-batch": the step
# without waiting, before the answers are posted, names every entry that
# awaits its marked answer, in identifier order.
# A marked answer to a hash emits no event. B and Z lost their last want
# during tick 5: each falls once the record of tick 6 is applied, unless a
# want names it by then. An answer that differs counts as an answer that
# differed whether or not it is applied.

scenario first-step-arrival/restore-with-the-parent
restore new-history differed B, P, Z
blocked 6 awaiting B, P, Z

# The content at hand holds B, P and Z at other hashes, and the part of P at
# hand lists B and Z. Each is answered as an entry the table holds
# available, by a marked answer; the two dependencies the part newly lists
# are entries the save holds available, so no ordinary arrival is posted.
# At the first tick the core first adds the want of B and the want of Z from
# the part of the answer of P, each owned by P and required as the want of P
# is. Then the answers, by identifier. The answer of B is applied before the
# answer of P, on an entry that a want added at the first step already
# names: B takes the hash at hand. P and Z take theirs. No event on a marked
# answer. Neither B nor Z falls. The three answers differed: the event of
# the restore, the one event of the tick, names the three.
tick 6
record arrival B B.2 marked
record arrival P P.2 marked
record arrival Z Z.2 marked
entry B parameters available B.2
want asset:P required
entry P parameters available P.2
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.2
want asset:P required
event new-history differed B, P, Z

# The table of tick 6 stands to tick 10, and the records of ticks 7 to 10
# are empty.


scenario first-step-arrival/restore-without-the-parent
restore new-history differed B, Z
blocked 6 awaiting B, P, Z

# The content at hand holds B and Z at other hashes and P at its recorded
# hash. A restore answers every entry the table holds available, so the
# answer of P stands in the record: it is at the hash in force, whose part
# lists nothing, and it changes nothing.
# The first step adds no want. The answers of B and of Z are for entries
# that no want names: neither is applied, and the table never takes the
# hashes at hand. Each differs from the recorded hash and counts as an
# answer that differed: the restore starts a new history, and its event
# names B and Z. Both entries then fall and leave.
tick 6
record arrival B B.2 marked
record arrival P P.1 marked
record arrival Z Z.2 marked
entry P parameters available P.1
want source:host required
event new-history differed B, Z

# The table of tick 6 stands to tick 10, and the records of ticks 7 to 10
# are empty.


scenario first-step-arrival/restore-at-depth-two
restore new-history differed P, Z
blocked 6 awaiting B, P, Z

# The content at hand holds P and Z at other hashes, and B at its recorded
# hash. The part of P at hand lists M, which the saved table does not name:
# the want the core adds for it creates its entry, so M is posted with the
# answers, as an ordinary arrival. The part of M lists Z, an entry the save
# holds available, which its marked answer answers.
# At the first tick the core adds the want of M from the part of the answer
# of P, owned by P and required, the first of M; M then holds a want, and
# the core adds the want of Z from the part of the arrival of M, owned by M
# and required. Then the arrivals, by identifier. The answer of B is at the
# recorded hash, for an entry no want names: it changes nothing. M rises,
# with its event, being no answer. P takes the hash at hand.
# Reading taken: the marked answer of Z is applied, a want added at the
# first step naming Z, although that want is added from the part of an
# ordinary arrival and not from the part of another answer. Z takes the
# hash at hand, with no event, and does not fall. B falls.
# The answers of P and of Z differed. M is no answer: the event of the
# restore does not name it.
tick 6
record arrival B B.1 marked
record arrival M M.1
record arrival P P.3 marked
record arrival Z Z.2 marked
entry M parameters available M.1
want asset:P required
entry P parameters available P.3
want source:host required
entry Z parameters available Z.2
want asset:M required
event new-history differed P, Z
event arrived M M.1

# The table of tick 6 stands to tick 10, and the records of ticks 7 to 10
# are empty.
