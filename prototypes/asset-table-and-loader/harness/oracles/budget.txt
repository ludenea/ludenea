# Oracles of the family "budget": an excess over a world's budget.
# Written by hand from the design text and from the definitions of the three
# candidates in the sheet, by a writer who has not read the build.
# Asset identifiers order as B, D, K, N, R, S, V, W, X.
# Record lines stand in the order applied: the commands first, in the order
# the scenario gives them, then the arrivals by asset identifier.
# An entry line with no want line under it is an entry that holds no want.
# A tick 0 block states the table the creation gives.
#
# Each case is stated three times, once per candidate, as the candidate's
# definition gives it. None of the three is chosen: the unit the founder took
# on 2026-11-14 is the fourth candidate, stated below the line, and the three
# are kept for comparison. Under the candidate on the closure of one want
# every arrival to a hash carries its mark; under the two others no arrival
# does.
#
# The count of a world is the sum of the sizes of the contents its table
# holds available. The budget is 10 000 bytes but where a comment says
# otherwise. Sizes: B.1 9 000, D.1 6 000, K.1 5 000, K.2 11 000, N.1 2 000,
# R.1 2 000, S.1 1 000, S.2 2 000, V.1 1 000, W.1 1 000, X.1 9 000.
#
# Reading taken by the founder on 2026-11-14, under which seven of the
# oracles of the three candidates are written again: the wants the core adds
# follow the parts in force. The core adds the wants the parts of a batch
# list before any arrival is tested; the arrivals are then applied or
# rejected in identifier order; the wants the core added are then recomputed
# from the parts the table holds available. A parent whose arrival is
# rejected and that is failed owns no want on its dependencies: those wants
# end at that fixed point, once the arrivals are gone through, and a
# dependency left with no want falls at the next tick as any asset does.
# The reading not taken, which those oracles first held: the wants stand
# whatever the test gives, for as long as the failed parent holds a want.
# The seven: "closure-over-the-budget" under the three candidates,
# "overlapping-closures" and "root-and-dependency" under the closure of one
# want and under the whole drain. The eleven others give the same states
# under both readings: where a dependency is rejected there, its parent is
# in force and lists it.
#
# Reading taken: a rejected first load is an answer, as any failure is. The
# gate opens on it, the step names a required asset, and the want does not
# leave the world again.
#
# Reading taken: an arrival is applied or rejected at its place in the
# identifier order with all that it does there. The step names a dependency
# that its rejected arrival fails under a required want, although that want
# ends afterwards at the same fixed point, with the parent that owned it:
# the rule on the wants moves the end of a want and nothing of an arrival.
# The other reading: the step names the failed assets that a required want
# still names once the fixed point is done, and so names no such dependency.
# The rule on the wants is silent between the two. Five oracles turn on it:
# the "names-failed" line of D in "closure-over-the-budget" and in
# "root-and-dependency", each under the closure of one want and under the
# whole drain, and the "names-failed" line of N in "overlapping-closures"
# under the whole drain.

# The fourth candidate, "derived-closure": the unit of the design text, the
# group of a root, with its count and its rule on the wants the core adds.
# Eight scenarios: the six cases above, and two cases that run under this
# candidate alone. Written by hand from the design text, by a writer who
# has not read the build. No state is taken from above this line.
#
# Written again for two things the design text has gained since these eight
# worlds first ran.
#
# The size. An arrival to a hash names the size of what it puts in force and
# the entry holds it: every arrival to a hash and every available entry
# states it here. A failed arrival and a failed entry hold none. The sizes
# are those of the sheet's table, which the domain manifest gives: B.1 9 000,
# D.1 6 000, K.1 5 000, K.2 11 000, N.1 2 000, R.1 2 000, R.2 2 000,
# S.1 1 000, S.2 2 000, V.1 1 000, W.1 1 000, X.1 9 000.
#
# The order of the test, the reading taken on 2026-11-14: the groups of the
# roots that hold a required want are tested first, in the identifier order
# of those roots, then the groups of the other roots, in the identifier
# order of theirs. The eight worlds first ran in the identifier order of the
# roots alone, which is now the reading not taken. Three worlds hold a root
# under an optional want that stands before a root under a required one.
# In "small-beside-large" and in "overlapping-closures" the groups are
# tested in another order and are decided as before: the states are the
# same. In "two-closures-that-fit-alone" the state changes.
#
# Nothing but the sizes changes in the seven other worlds. No record of the
# eight holds an arrival to failed, and no group of the eight leaves the
# count as it was or lowers it, so the two sentences on the count and on a
# group that does not raise it decide nothing here.
#
# At a fixed point, in this order: the commands of the record; the groups,
# derived and each decided before any want of the batch is added and before
# any arrival is applied; the wants that the parts of the accepted arrivals
# list, and those alone; the arrivals in identifier order, a rejected one
# that fails its asset doing so at its own place; the wants the core added,
# recomputed from the parts in force; the falls.
#
# The closure of a root is read from the part in force and from the part of
# the arrival, from both where an asset has both. The wants that a rejected
# part lists are not added: an asset that a rejected part alone lists is
# named by no want, and its rejected arrival leaves no entry, no failure and
# no event. The result of the step names every rejected arrival all the
# same, as a "rejected" line.
#
# The batch of a revision holds an arrival for each asset the revision
# changes that the table names and for each asset of the closure of a
# changed part that the table does not hold available, and for no other.
#
# A want made on an entry the table holds available is answered already,
# and no arrival follows it.
