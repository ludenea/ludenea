# Oracles of the family "sizes": the size an arrival names and an entry
# holds gives the count of a world.
# Written by hand from the design text, by a writer who has not read the build.
# Asset identifiers order as H, K, N, S, W.
# Record lines stand in the order applied: the lowering of a budget, or the
# commands in the order the scenario gives them, then the arrivals by asset
# identifier. No record of this family holds both a lowering and a command.
# An entry line with no want line under it is an entry that holds no want.
# A tick 0 block states the table the creation gives.
#
# Every arrival to a hash names the size of what it puts in force, as the
# domain manifest gives it, and every available entry holds that size. A
# failed arrival and a failed entry hold none. Sizes in bytes: H.1 4 000,
# K.1 5 000, K.2 7 000, K.3 3 000, N.1 2 000, S.1 1 000, W.1 1 000. The
# part of H.1 is 1 000 bytes long: its size is not the length of its part.
# No part lists a dependency.
#
# The count of a world is the sum of the sizes its available entries hold,
# less an entry that falls at the same fixed point. It is a function of the
# table: a restored table has the count of the table that was saved, and a
# replay derives every rejection again from the record and from the state.
# A rejected arrival stays in the record as an arrival to a hash, with its
# size; the failure of a rejected first load is in no record.
#
# The budget of a world is no state. Reading taken: a restored world stands
# under the budget its save holds, the lowered one, whichever restore it is.
# Reading taken: the lowering of a budget is applied before the arrivals of
# its record. Here the record that holds it holds nothing else.
#
# The order of the test is the one taken on 2026-11-14, the roots that hold
# a required want first. No tick of this family holds two groups whose order
# changes a state.

# Scenario "run-and-replay". A budget of 12 000 bytes at creation, lowered
# to 10 000 bytes by the record of tick 4.

scenario sizes/run-and-replay

# The world that runs over a live loading capability and writes the log.
world recorded

want contributor:base required
tick 1

# A rejected replacement. The batch of the revision holds the new hash of K

# Scenario "restored-at-a-smaller-hash". The content at hand holds K.3,
# 3 000 bytes, in place of K.1, and the part of K.1 is not at hand. Every
# entry in force is answered by a marked arrival in the record of the first
# tick; the failed entry, under a want that stands and on which no want is
# added, receives nothing. The answer of K differs: the restore starts a new
# history.

scenario sizes/restored-at-a-smaller-hash
restore new-history differed K

# The answers of H and of S are at the hash their entries hold: they leave
# the entries as they were and stand in no group. The answer of K is at
# another hash: it stands in the group of K, and the count takes its size in

# part not being at hand: 10 000, less 5 000, and 3 000, is 8 000, within,
